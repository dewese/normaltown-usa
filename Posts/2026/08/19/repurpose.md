## LinkedIn
Took me too long to understand this: a health insurer makes more money when it pays out less. Not a scandal, just how the business is built.

Picture a referee on the field, except one of the teams signs his paycheck. Even an honest ref now has a thumb on the scale. When you file a claim, the company deciding whether to pay is the same company that keeps the money if it doesn't. Referee and one of the teams, at once.

You've felt it: the technicality denial, the "not pre-approved," the bill that bounces three times. A dollar they don't pay is a dollar they keep, so the process is built with lots of doors.

It's why some people look at setups where the folks handling the money don't profit by keeping yours. Honest breakdown in comments.
