## LinkedIn
An HSA might be the most useful money tool people barely understand. Here it is in plain English.

Picture a jar labeled "health." The government agrees not to tax the money you put in it. And as long as you spend it on health, it comes out untaxed too. Money in, no tax. Money out for health, no tax. Most accounts get taxed on one end; this one dodges both.

Two things people get wrong:
- It's not use-it-or-lose-it. Unspent money rolls over and stays yours, even if you change jobs.
- You can't just open one. You need a high-deductible health plan first, so the two come as a pair.

That pair fits healthier folks who rarely hit the big deductible, and fits worse for those with heavy ongoing needs. Full breakdown in comments. (Not tax advice; check current figures.)

## Facebook / Instagram
Ever heard of an HSA and nodded along without really knowing what it is? Here's the plain version.

It's a jar labeled "health" that the tax man agrees not to touch. Money in, no tax. Money out for health, no tax. And it rolls over every year as your own money, even if you switch jobs.

The catch: you need a high-deductible health plan to open one, so it fits healthier folks better than those with heavy medical needs. If it fits you, it's one of the best deals around. Full plain-English guide at the link in bio.
