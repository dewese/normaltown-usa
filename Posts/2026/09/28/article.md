# Letting Go of the "What If"

> No coverage on earth erases risk. Insurance doesn't, health sharing doesn't, being rich doesn't. The goal isn't zero worry. It's turning the vague "what if" into a concrete plan: pay my $500, let the advocate negotiate, get care first in an emergency, cover gaps with the emergency fund, pay cash for routine. Do the controllable part well, then set the rest down.

I want to talk about the quiet thing underneath all these health and money decisions. It's not really about premiums or deductibles or crowd funding. It's about the "what if" that runs in the back of your mind. What if something big happens? What if I picked wrong? That worry is heavy, and I've carried it too. So let me talk honestly about how to set it down, whatever coverage you end up choosing.

## The "what if" never fully goes away, and that's okay

Here's the first honest thing. No coverage on earth erases risk. Insurance doesn't. Health sharing doesn't. Being rich doesn't. Life comes with uncertainty, and any product promising to make you totally safe is lying to you.

So the goal was never zero worry. The goal is to worry about the right size of thing, and to know you've done the reasonable stuff a responsible person can do. Once you've done that, the leftover worry is just the tax we all pay for being alive, and you're allowed to put it down.

## Trade the vague fear for a concrete plan

Vague worry is the worst kind, because your brain fills the blank with the scariest possible movie. The fix is to make the fear concrete, because a concrete problem has a concrete answer.

So instead of "what if something awful happens," write down the actual plan:

- If a health event hits, I pay my $500, and my advocate negotiates the bill down.
- If it's an emergency, I get care first and sort the paperwork after.
- If there's a gap, my emergency fund covers it.
- If it's routine, I pay the cash price.

Suddenly the monster under the bed is just a list. Lists aren't scary. I built my own version of that list as [a layered safety net](https://www.normaltownusa.com/p/the-diy-healthcare-safety-net).

## Control what you can, release what you can't

There's real freedom in sorting your worries into two buckets.

In the "I can control this" bucket: keeping $500 liquid, reading the guidelines, asking for the cash price, choosing coverage that fits your health honestly. Do these, fully, and take the win.

In the "I can't control this" bucket: whether you get sick, when, and the exact shape of the future. You cannot think your way to certainty about these, so spending your peace on them is just paying twice. Do the first bucket well, and you earn the right to release the second.

## Why this matters for your actual health

This isn't just feel-good talk. Chronic worry about money and health is its own kind of tax on your body and your sleep. Part of why my family landed where we did is that a simple, understandable plan let us stop white-knuckling it. Knowing the shape of the deal, limits and all, is what let me actually relax. That peace is a real benefit, not a soft one.

## The takeaway

You will never buy your way to zero risk, so stop trying. Instead, do the reasonable things a responsible person can do, turn the vague "what if" into a concrete plan, and then genuinely let the rest go. You've done your part. Carrying the worry on top of the plan doesn't make you safer. It just makes you tired. Set it down.
