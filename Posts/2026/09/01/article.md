If you have ever tried to figure out what your health insurance really costs, you know the problem. There is the premium you pay every month, the deductible you have to hit before it helps, the copays, the coinsurance, and the surprise bill that shows up anyway. Health sharing works differently, and the money side is a lot easier to say out loud. Here is the honest math.

## The two numbers that matter

With CrowdHealth, the health sharing service I use and point people to, there are really two numbers.

The first is a flat **$60 a month per person**. That is the advocacy fee. It pays for the app, the bill negotiators, and a real human who helps you when something goes wrong. It does not change based on how sick you are or how old you are.

The second is a **$500 member commitment per health event**. Think of a health event as one thing that happens to you, like a broken arm or an appendix that has to come out. You cover the first $500 of that event. The community covers the rest of the eligible bill.

That is the whole shape of it. A flat monthly fee, and $500 when something big happens.

## The part I want to be straight about

There is a third piece, and I would be doing you a disservice to skip it. On top of the $60, you also chip in a small amount each month to help fund other members' bills. That is the "sharing" part. It moves up and down depending on what the community needs that month, and you approve each request. It is usually modest, but it is not zero, so your real monthly number is a bit more than $60.

Even with that, most healthy people and families land well below what a traditional plan costs. But I want you comparing real numbers, not a fantasy.

## Why this is easier to plan for

Picture two phone bills. One is a flat plan where you know the number every month. The other charges you a base rate, plus overage fees, plus a device fee, plus taxes you cannot predict. Same service, totally different stress level.

Traditional insurance is the second bill. You pay the premium, and then you still owe a deductible that can run five figures before the plan pays a dime. Health sharing is closer to the first bill. A flat fee, a known amount per event, and negotiators working to shrink the bill before the crowd ever funds it. Members regularly see cash-pay discounts of 25 to 85 percent off the sticker price.

## Who this math works for

This works best if you are relatively healthy, you have $500 set aside for a health event, and you want a lower, more predictable monthly number. It does not work for everyone. It is not insurance, there is no legal guarantee your bill gets funded, and there are real exclusions and rules for pre-existing conditions.
