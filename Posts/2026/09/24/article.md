# Health Sharing vs. COBRA

> COBRA keeps your exact work plan after you leave a job, but you pay the full price your employer used to hide, often well over a thousand dollars a month for a family. If you're healthy and just bridging to the next job, health sharing usually does it for a fraction. If you're mid-treatment or need an ironclad guarantee, keep COBRA.

If you've ever lost or left a job, you've met COBRA, and you probably remember the sticker shock. COBRA lets you keep your old work health insurance for a while after you leave, which sounds great until you see the price. Suddenly you're paying the full cost your employer used to hide from you, and it's brutal. When that letter shows up, health sharing is worth a serious look. Let me compare them honestly.

## What COBRA actually is

At your job, your employer quietly pays a big chunk of your health insurance. You only ever saw your small slice on the pay stub. COBRA lets you keep that exact same plan after you leave, but now you pay the whole thing yourself, employer's share included, plus a little admin fee on top.

So the coverage doesn't change. The price just triples or worse, because the hidden subsidy is gone. For a family, COBRA can easily run well over a thousand dollars a month, sometimes closer to two.

## The case for COBRA

I'll give it its due. COBRA has real advantages in specific situations:

- **It's the same plan and network,** so your doctors and any ongoing treatment continue seamlessly.
- **It's real insurance,** with a legal guarantee and your accumulated deductible for the year.
- **It's the right call mid-treatment.** If you're in the middle of a serious health situation, do not switch away from real coverage to save money. Keep COBRA.

COBRA exists for continuity, and continuity is exactly what you want when something is already going on.

## The case for health sharing instead

But if you're healthy and just need a bridge to your next job or plan, COBRA's price is punishing for what you get. Here's where health sharing shines:

- **It's not tied to a job you no longer have,** so it doesn't end when your COBRA window runs out.

For a healthy person between jobs, paying COBRA prices can feel like lighting money on fire.

## The honest deciding factor

It really comes down to two questions. Are you healthy right now, and can you handle a bit of risk for a big cost savings? If yes, health sharing is often the smarter bridge. Are you mid-treatment or do you need an ironclad guarantee right now? Then COBRA's price buys you something real, and it's worth it. Because health sharing is not insurance, there's no legal guarantee, so this choice really hinges on your current health. ## The takeaway

COBRA keeps your exact plan but charges you the brutal full price, which makes sense only when you need continuity, like being mid-treatment. If you're healthy and just bridging a gap between jobs, health sharing usually does the job for a fraction of the cost, and it isn't tied to the job you left. Match the tool to your situation, and don't overpay for a guarantee you may not need.
