# Tonsils, Ear Tubes, and Other Kid Surgeries

> One family's tonsillectomy was funded by the CrowdHealth crowd, but a second procedure for the same child never was, because the hospital wouldn't provide a written price estimate. CrowdHealth exists because its founder got an $8,000 bill for his daughter's 15-minute ear tube surgery after insurance refused to pay. Kid surgeries are common, planned, and very shoppable.

If you have kids, you've probably sat in a waiting room for one of these. Tonsils. Ear tubes. Adenoids. They're short surgeries with long bills.

## What happened?

**The ear tubes that started it all.** This one isn't a member story. It's the reason CrowdHealth exists. Its founder had a one-year-old daughter with ear infections that kept coming back and a damaged eardrum. She needed ear tubes, a 15-minute surgery.

He was paying about $1,200 a month for insurance. The insurance company decided the surgery wasn't medically necessary. He appealed twice and lost twice. He paid the $8,000 himself.

Later he worked out that the cash price would have been around $2,000, maybe less. He'd paid for insurance and then paid four times the cash price anyway.

**The tonsils and the scope.** A parent wrote a three-star review about their son, who needed his tonsils out and also needed a scope procedure. The two couldn't be done together.

The tonsillectomy and its bills were funded. The scope wasn't. The hold-up was a document called a Good Faith Estimate. That's a written price a provider is required to give patients who are paying directly. This family lives where one hospital system runs nearly everything, and they could never get one.

The parent was fair about it. They said it wasn't really CrowdHealth's fault, though they might have gotten more help.

**The quick ones.** One parent's son had adenoid surgery, and CrowdHealth sent him a get-well gift. Another parent called their son's surgery their first big medical event and said it went great. A third said a daughter's procedure took a while to fund, and that the bills came down to a small fraction of the original cost.

## What do the bills look like?

CrowdHealth's public results list funded requests for exactly these: ear infections with ear tubes, sent to 78 members, and a tonsillectomy, sent to 19.

Kid surgeries are a lot like getting your brakes done. It's a standard job that shops do all day. The price should be easy to get up front. When a place won't tell you the price before the work, that tells you something about the place.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

For the tonsils, the normal thing: funded the bills above $500. For the scope, they needed a written estimate to work from, and without one they were stuck.

## What's the honest part?

That scope story is the real lesson. CrowdHealth runs on prices. If a hospital refuses to put a price in writing, the whole process jams. And in a town with one hospital system, you can't just go across the street.

So before you join, ask yourself what's near you. Is there an independent surgery center? An ear, nose, and throat doctor who posts cash prices? If the answer is no, planned surgeries get harder.

Each surgery is also its own $500, per child, when they're separate problems. Two kids with two different surgeries in one year is $1,000 from you.

And well-child checkups are mostly on you. One wellness visit a year per child is shared, up to $300. Little kids have a lot more than one. Starting in 2027 that $300 can be spread across several visits, but it's still $300. One dad who joined with three kids counted those extra visits as a real out-of-pocket cost when he ran his numbers.

CrowdHealth's founder also warns members off children's hospital ERs for small things like stitches and fevers, because the prices can be several times higher.

## The takeaway

A 15-minute ear surgery cost one insured dad $8,000, and he built a company because of it. For members today, these surgeries usually go fine: $500 from you, the rest from the crowd. Where it breaks is when a hospital won't name its price. Find out now who near you will.

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth cover ear tubes and tonsillectomies?

Yes, when the problem is new since joining. CrowdHealth's public results show funded requests for both. You pay the first $500 of each health event. Ask the surgeon's office and the facility for written cash prices before the surgery date.

### What is a Good Faith Estimate?

It's a written estimate of what your care will cost, which providers are required to give to patients who aren't using insurance. CrowdHealth uses it to check the price and fund planned procedures. One family couldn't get one from their hospital, and that procedure went unfunded.

### Are kids' checkups covered?

One wellness visit per child per year is shared, up to $300. Babies and toddlers have several well visits a year, so the rest are yours at the cash price. See [a healthy family's year on health sharing](/p/a-healthy-familys-year-on-health-sharing/) for how that adds up.
