# The MRI That Took Two Weeks Instead of Months

> One member waited months for insurance to approve a shoulder MRI. On CrowdHealth it took under two weeks and cost about half the usual price. Another family saved more than $5,000 on a heart MRI for their son after their advocate found a better-priced place. Imaging is where shopping around pays off the most.

An MRI is a big scanning machine that takes pictures of the inside of your body. It's also one of the wildest-priced things in all of medicine. The same scan can cost a few hundred dollars or several thousand, depending on the building.

## What happened?

**The shoulder.** A member needed a shoulder MRI and spent months trying to get it approved under their old coverage. After switching to CrowdHealth, they had the scan in under two weeks. The bill was about 50% lower than the going rate.

**The son's heart.** A family that was new to CrowdHealth needed a cardiac MRI for their son. Their advocate found a price that saved them more than $5,000 compared with what they'd been looking at. The same family had two wellness visits funded too.

**The frozen shoulder.** Another member, on a first-ever health event, needed an MRI and later a special injection for a frozen shoulder. The advocate found one provider that could do both.

**The knee.** A woman hurt her knee a few months after enrolling, while she still had her old insurance as a backup. Going through that insurance was going to cost her upwards of $10,000. CrowdHealth's team lined up the surgeon and the imaging quickly. Afterward her family dropped the insurance.

**The extra $700.** A member wrote in a members' group that he'd already gotten a discounted cash price on an MRI by asking. Then CrowdHealth got it another $700 lower.

## What does the bill look like?

Here's why imaging is such a good deal to shop for. An MRI is the same machine doing the same thing everywhere. It isn't like surgery, where the surgeon's skill matters a lot. A standalone imaging center takes the same kind of picture a hospital does.

But hospitals often charge several times more. It's like bottled water. Same water at the gas station, the ballpark, and the airport. Wildly different prices, and the only thing that changed is where you were standing.

In CrowdHealth's public results, an MRI request is usually small. One went to 17 members. Several went to fewer than 10.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

Two things, and both are simple. They knew where the fair-priced places were, and there was no approval line to wait in.

That second one surprised me. With insurance, a scan often needs sign-off first, and that can take weeks. Here, you and your doctor decide you need a scan, you find a fair price, and you go.

## What's the honest part?

If your MRI costs less than $500, it's all yours. Plenty of cash-price scans come in under that, so some members pay for imaging entirely out of pocket and the crowd never gets involved. You're still better off at the cash price. Just don't expect a check.

One member had a bad time with this exact thing. A testing bill came to $850, and they still paid $500 of it. They said it made them doubt whether a real emergency would go well. CrowdHealth's reply was that on a deductible plan they'd have paid the full $850. That's true, and I also understand why $350 back didn't feel like much.

The imaging also has to be for a new problem. A problem you had before joining can fall under the pre-existing rule.

## The takeaway

For scans, the win usually isn't the crowd paying. It's finding out the scan costs a third of what you thought, and getting it next week instead of next season. Ask for the cash price at a standalone imaging center. That one habit will save you money on any plan.

## Questions I get about this

### How much does an MRI cost without insurance?

It depends hugely on where you go. Members report bills about 50% under the usual rate, and one family saved over $5,000 on a cardiac MRI by switching locations. Standalone imaging centers are usually much cheaper than hospitals. More in [why cash prices beat insured prices](/p/why-cash-prices-beat-insured-prices/).

### Do I need approval before getting a scan on CrowdHealth?

There's no insurance-style approval step. For planned care, CrowdHealth asks you to open the event in the app first so your advocate can help find a fair price. Skipping that step can affect how a bill is scored.

### Will the crowd pay for my MRI?

Only the part above your $500 for that health event. If the scan is part of a bigger event, like a knee injury with surgery, everything counts together. If it's a $400 scan and nothing else, you pay the $400.
