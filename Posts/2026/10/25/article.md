# The Freak Accident: Three Hospitals and a Helicopter

> A CrowdHealth member was accidentally shot on a fishing trip, flown out by helicopter, and treated at three hospitals over several months. By his own estimate the bills ran somewhere between $500,000 and $1 million. CrowdHealth negotiated and funded them. The largest bill on CrowdHealth's public list is a firearm injury at $643,242.

Insurance people call this "catastrophic." I call it the thing you actually buy protection for. Everything else is just budgeting.

## What happened?

A man who hadn't seen a doctor in about 20 years was out fishing, far from any town. He was carrying a handgun in a holster. It fell, hit a rock, and went off.

One bullet. Six holes. It went through his leg twice and through his body.

His wife made a tourniquet and called for a helicopter. He was flown to a hospital, passed out, and woke up about three days later. He went home after a little over a week. Then came a fever of 104 or 105, a serious blood infection, and another two weeks in the hospital. Later, one more hospital stay and another surgery. Three hospitals in all.

He says he's back to about 90%. He runs, hikes, lifts, and fishes.

## What did the bill look like?

I have to be careful here, because the numbers don't agree with each other.

The member himself said the first hospital's bill was close to $300,000. He figured the total was more than $500,000 and less than a million, and admitted he never added it all up.

CrowdHealth's founder has told the story as roughly a million dollars, negotiated down to somewhere between $200,000 and $250,000.

And the top bill on CrowdHealth's public list is $643,242 for a firearm injury. That may or may not be the same event. CrowdHealth doesn't say.

So I'll just say this: hundreds of thousands of dollars, at least.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

He took a photo of each bill as it arrived and uploaded it in the app. CrowdHealth negotiated them down and the crowd funded them. The founder called him personally to say they'd take care of him.

There's one detail I can't shake. He was so worried about cost that he thought about taking out his own surgical drains at home to save money. Someone at CrowdHealth talked him out of it and told him to go get proper care.

When a person is thinking about that, medical bills have stopped being a money problem. It's like a house fire where the owner is worrying about the water bill.

## Are there other accident stories?

A few.

A young woman on her parents' membership lost two fingers in a water sports accident. Her family opened the event with CrowdHealth from the ER. She had surgery that same night with a hand surgeon. CrowdHealth sent a care package, and her parent said the case was being left open in case she wants prosthetics later. There were no dollar figures in that one.

A member who hadn't seen a doctor in years had a freak accident and spent more than two weeks in the hospital. They wrote early, before the bills were settled, and praised how fast the team responded.

Another had a bike wreck and one night in the hospital, and said the process was easier and faster than expected.

CrowdHealth's list also shows a car accident at $267,636 and a broken leg at $180,761. And in its October 2026 newsletter, CrowdHealth told members the crowd had recently taken on two much larger events, one of nearly $3 million and another of $1.3 million. It didn't say what they were.

## What's the honest part?

The dollar figures on the big story are fuzzy, and I've shown you why. Three sources, three different totals. Treat any single number with suspicion, including the ones I just gave you.

A bill this size also gets split into many requests over many months. CrowdHealth's results show more than two dozen separate requests for one firearm injury across about half a year. This wasn't a one-week process.

Some accidents aren't eligible at all. CrowdHealth's guide excludes injuries from a list of hazardous activities, injuries while under the influence of alcohol or drugs, injuries during illegal acts, and self-inflicted ones.

And no promise was made in writing. He got a phone call from the founder. That's kind, and it isn't a contract.

## The takeaway

This is the story I'd point to if someone asked, "But what about something really bad?" A man nearly died, three hospitals billed him six figures each, and the crowd handled it. The exact totals are murky. The outcome isn't. He's fishing again.

## Questions I get about this

### Is there a limit on how much CrowdHealth will fund?

CrowdHealth says there's no yearly or lifetime maximum on eligible bills, and its public list shows funded bills up to $643,242. Funding is never guaranteed, and members are responsible for their own bills in the end. See [can you trust health sharing with a big bill](/p/can-you-trust-health-sharing-with-a-big-bill/).

### Are accident injuries covered from day one?

New accidents are eligible right away, with no waiting period. You pay the first $500 of the event. Injuries from certain hazardous activities, or while under the influence, are excluded.

### How does a huge bill get funded by a crowd?

It's negotiated down first, then split into many requests sent to different groups of members over time. Each member's monthly share has a ceiling, so nobody gets a giant charge.
