# Broken Bones: The Ankle, the Elbow, and the First Confusing Day

> A member broke an ankle less than a month after joining CrowdHealth and needed surgery. Staff helped find a surgeon who takes direct-pay patients and funded the surgery ahead of time. Another family's son broke his elbow, and their first day or two was confusing until one phone call. Injuries are the biggest share of what the crowd funds, 44% in September 2026.

Nobody plans a broken bone. Injuries made up 44% of the health events in CrowdHealth's September 2026 results, and 48.9% the month before. So if you join, this is the kind of thing you're most likely to use it for.

## What happened?

**The ankle.** A brand-new member, less than a month in, broke an ankle badly enough to need surgery. Two people at CrowdHealth helped find a doctor who works with patients paying directly. They also coached the member on exactly what to ask for in the price estimate.

Then they did the thing I find most reassuring. They funded the surgery before it happened. The member didn't have to pay thousands and wait.

**The elbow.** A couple who'd been raised to never, ever go without insurance joined with a lot of hesitation. Then their son broke his elbow. They went to the ER, and a follow-up showed he'd need surgery. The cash estimate was, in their word, "staggering."

They were honest that the first day or two was rough. They didn't know what to do, and the app wasn't much help in the moment. One phone call with their advocate cleared it up. They finished the review saying they felt at ease.

**The quick ones.** A member who broke an arm right after joining said every penny past the commitment was covered. A couple of two years said the husband's broken wrist cost them less than it would have on insurance. And one member with a broken ankle got a meal gift card and a mug in the mail that said "I do my own stunts."

## What do the bills look like?

Bigger than you'd guess. Most of these reviews don't give totals, but CrowdHealth's public results do show which requests topped $10,000. Among them: a broken wrist from a scooter crash that went to 72 members, a fractured elbow that went to 46, a double broken wrist that went to 42, and a broken ankle that went to 33.

A broken bone that needs surgery is a five-figure bill pretty often. Your share is $500.

Here's how I think about that $500. It's like the deposit on a rental truck. It's enough that you'll take care, and small enough that it won't wreck your month.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

Found a surgeon, taught the member what to ask, and paid before the surgery. For the elbow family, it was mostly one clear phone call that turned panic into a to-do list.

## What's the honest part?

That first-day confusion is real, and I've seen it in more than one review. Another member who tore a knee ligament on a weekend said the same thing: it would have been easier to learn the process on something small. There's no card to hand the front desk. You have to know to say "I'm paying directly, what's your cash price?"

So learn the steps before you need them. Save your advocate's contact info in your phone the day you join.

Also know the fine print on risky hobbies. CrowdHealth's guide excludes injuries from a list of hazardous activities, like motorcycle racing and skydiving, and injuries that happen while under the influence. Ordinary bike wrecks show up in the funded results. Skydiving is on the excluded list.

## The takeaway

Broken bones are the bread and butter of what this crowd pays for, and the stories are steady: you pay $500, the crowd handles the rest, and sometimes the surgery is funded before you roll in. The weak spot is the first 48 hours for a first-timer. That's fixable with ten minutes of homework.

## Questions I get about this

### What do I do first if my kid breaks a bone?

Get care. Then open a health event in the app or message your advocate, and tell the provider you're paying directly and want the cash price. Ask for itemized bills. I laid out the steps in [health sharing and the ER](/p/health-sharing-and-the-er/).

### Will CrowdHealth pay for surgery before it happens?

It can. One member's ankle surgery was funded ahead of time using an estimate from the surgeon. CrowdHealth says planned, larger procedures can be negotiated and funded before the date, so you aren't floating the whole bill.

### Are sports injuries covered?

Ordinary ones, yes. Public results show funded requests for football and basketball concussions, bike wrecks, and broken wrists. The exclusions are for a specific list of hazardous activities and for injuries while impaired. Check the list in [what CrowdHealth doesn't cover](/p/what-crowdhealth-doesnt-cover/).
