# A New Hip for $500 (and Why It Took Some Work)

> A self-employed CrowdHealth member needed a hip replacement. By having it done at an outpatient surgery center instead of a hospital, the facility charge was one third of the hospital price, and he paid $500. He also turned down the first surgeon suggested and says his own effort was key. It isn't a magic fix, and he says so.

Joint replacements are planned. You see them coming for months. That makes them the best case for this model, because there's time to shop.

## What happened?

CrowdHealth published the story of a self-employed man who'd started several businesses. He needed a new hip. It was his first big health event as a member.

It didn't go in a straight line. Getting an X-ray order from his regular doctor turned into a hassle. Then CrowdHealth suggested a surgery center in another city, and that first surgeon didn't meet his standards. So he pushed back.

They kept working at it together. They found a hip specialist from a well-regarded local group who operates at an outpatient surgery center near his home. That's a place built for same-day surgeries, separate from a hospital.

## What did the bill look like?

The story doesn't give the total. It gives the two numbers that matter:

- **Facility charge at the surgery center:** one third of the hospital price
- **What he paid:** $500

Same new hip. One third the building cost.

A hospital is like a full-service hotel. You're paying for the lobby, the ballroom, and the 24-hour everything, whether you use them or not. An outpatient center is the clean roadside motel. You get the bed and the hot shower, and you don't pay for the ballroom.

## Is there a second story?

Yes, and it isn't from CrowdHealth's own site. A member posted on an independent review site about a knee replacement. They paid $500, on top of a regular monthly cost of $176. CrowdHealth found a private surgery clinic about an hour from home. Physical therapy afterward was paid back in full. The second knee was already scheduled.

CrowdHealth's public results also show a hip replacement request that went out to 132 members.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

Found the fair price for the surgery, suggested surgeons, and kept looking when the first one wasn't a fit. Then the crowd funded everything over $500.

## What's the honest part?

This member was blunt about it, and CrowdHealth printed it anyway, which I give them credit for. The piece says outright that CrowdHealth "isn't a magic fix."

He said his own effort made the difference. He asked questions. He turned down a surgeon. He kept pushing until the plan was right.

He also said it might not suit people with frequent health problems, or people who'd rather be passive about their care.

That's the trade in one sentence. You save a pile of money, and in exchange you act like the customer. If you want to hand over a card and never think about it again, a surgery like this will frustrate you.

There's a second catch for joints specifically. Worn-out hips and knees usually hurt for years before surgery. If you had symptoms or treatment in the five years before joining, that joint is a pre-existing condition. It wouldn't be shared for your first two years, and it's capped at $25,000 a year after that. A lot of people shopping for coverage because their knee already hurts will run into this.

And the knee member drove an hour. Not everyone can.

## The takeaway

A new hip for $500 is real. So is the work behind it. For a planned surgery, the money is in where it's done, and an outpatient center can cost a fraction of a hospital. Bring your questions and your patience, and don't be afraid to say "not that surgeon."

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth cover hip or knee replacement?

If the joint problem is new since you joined, yes. Members have reported paying $500 for a hip replacement and $500 for a knee replacement. If you had symptoms or treatment in the five years before joining, the pre-existing rule applies.

### Why is an outpatient surgery center cheaper than a hospital?

Lower overhead. A surgery center does one kind of work and doesn't carry the cost of an ER or intensive care. One member's facility charge there was a third of the hospital's. The same idea is behind [why cash prices beat insured prices](/p/why-cash-prices-beat-insured-prices/).

### Can I say no to the surgeon CrowdHealth suggests?

Yes. There are no networks, so you choose. One member turned down the first suggestion and worked with CrowdHealth to find another. The price still has to be near a fair local rate for the crowd to fund all of it.
