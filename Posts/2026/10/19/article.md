# The ER Bill: $4,142 Became $1,657 With One Phone Call

> A dad took his toddler to the ER for croup and got a bill for $4,142.75. CrowdHealth told him the hospital hadn't applied its self-pay discount. He called, and the bill dropped 60% to $1,657.10. He paid $500, the crowd funded the rest, and the money was in his account two weeks after he uploaded the bill.

ER visits are the most common "big" bill a normal family runs into. So here are three of them, with real numbers.

## What happened?

**The toddler with croup.** A dad wrote on his own blog about his young son waking up in the night struggling to breathe. They went to the ER. It was croup, a swelling in the airway that's common in little kids. They were sent home a couple of hours later.

The bill showed up several weeks afterward: $4,142.75.

He uploaded it. Within a day or two, someone at CrowdHealth told him the hospital had overcharged and hadn't applied a self-pay discount. So he called the hospital and asked. They took 60% off right there on the phone.

**The $10,000 visit.** A self-employed mom wrote that her family of four dropped insurance this past year. Her husband landed in the ER in January. The visit was $10,000. The crowd paid all of it except their $500.

She also needed follow-up scans after a routine checkup. She figured insurance would have left her with around $4,000. On CrowdHealth, again, $500.

**The overnight stay.** A healthy member, four or five months in, had an ER visit and one night in the hospital. They paid the first $500. The biggest bill got negotiated to about half. Then they watched the crowd's money arrive in their account in small pieces: $62.45, $98.28, $76.13, $115.87.

## What do the bills look like?

Here's the croup visit, start to finish:

- **Billed:** $4,142.75
- **After the self-pay discount:** $1,657.10
- **Dad paid:** $500
- **Crowd funded:** about $1,157
- **Time from upload to money in the bank:** two weeks

I love that third story's detail about the small deposits. That's what a crowd looks like from the inside. It's a tip jar that fills up a few bills at a time until it covers the check.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

In the croup story, they spotted the overcharge. The dad made the call himself. That's worth being clear about. CrowdHealth told him what to ask. He did the asking.

In the overnight story, they coached the member on asking for self-pay discounts, negotiated the largest bill, and deposited the money once it was funded. That part took a day or two, maybe three.

## What's the honest part?

That 60% discount wasn't a hard-fought deal. It was a standard discount the hospital gives anyone paying directly, if they ask. The hospital just didn't offer it. You could get that same discount with no membership in anything.

Which tells you two things. One, always ask. Two, the value here was someone who knew to tell him to ask.

ER bills also come in pieces. One member noted that the hospital, the doctor, and the lab each send their own, and every one has to be forwarded. A blogger whose husband went in with stomach pain got four separate bills. The small one was funded within days. The big one was still being negotiated when she wrote about it.

And CrowdHealth's founder has a warning I'll pass along. A children's hospital ER can charge far more than a regular one. He described a family billed more than $6,500 for a dehydrated child who needed fluids. For stitches or a fever, he says, go somewhere else if you safely can.

## The takeaway

Three ER visits, three members who paid $500. The pattern I see: the first bill is too high, somebody asks, the bill drops, and the crowd splits what's left. The asking is the magic. Do it on any plan you have.

## Questions I get about this

### How much is an ER visit without insurance?

It varies a lot. In these member stories, one was $4,142.75 before a 60% discount and another was $10,000. Always ask for the self-pay price and an itemized bill. My full walk-through is in [health sharing and the ER](/p/health-sharing-and-the-er/).

### How do I get a self-pay discount at a hospital?

Call the billing office, say you're paying directly with no insurance, and ask what discount they offer. One dad got 60% off with a single call. I wrote out a script in [how to negotiate a hospital bill yourself](/p/how-to-negotiate-a-hospital-bill-yourself/).

### How long until I get paid back for an ER bill?

In these stories, between a few days and two weeks once the bill was approved. CrowdHealth's August 2026 average was 7.72 days from a complete bill to funds in the account. Larger hospital bills can take much longer to negotiate first.
