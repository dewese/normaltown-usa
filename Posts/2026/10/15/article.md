# His Heart Stopped for Four Minutes

> A member's heart stopped for more than four minutes at a public meeting. Medics revived him. He was flown to a hospital, spent a week in intensive care, and had triple bypass surgery. His wife called CrowdHealth the next day. When she wrote about it, many bills had been funded and others were still being negotiated.

This is about as big as a health event gets. It's also an unfinished story, and I'm going to tell it that way.

## What happened?

A wife wrote a public review this spring about her husband. They'd had regular insurance their whole lives until about two years ago, when they switched to CrowdHealth. In two years they hadn't needed it once.

Then her husband's heart stopped at a meeting. He was gone for over four minutes. Medics on the scene brought him back. He was flown to the hospital, spent a week in the ICU, and then had triple bypass surgery. That's open-heart surgery to route blood around three blocked arteries.

She called CrowdHealth the day after it happened to ask what she needed to do.

## What did CrowdHealth say?

Not to worry about any of it yet. Focus on him.

That same message shows up in more than one of the scary stories I read. When you're in a hospital hallway, the money question is sitting on your chest. Somebody telling you "that part can wait" is worth a lot.

After that, the team walked the two of them through the process. They'd never turned in a bill before, so they needed the hand-holding.

## What did the bill look like?

She didn't share numbers. She did list what was coming: the medical flight, the ICU, the surgeries, and more.

She said they were "beyond nervous" about whether it would all get paid. Two years of paying in, never taking out, and then the biggest possible bill all at once.

By the time she wrote, many of the bills had been crowdfunded. CrowdHealth was still negotiating others.

## Is there a second story like this?

Yes. A man who'd lost the insurance he had through work joined CrowdHealth, then had a stroke that led to heart surgery. His path to treatment ran through several hospital systems and took almost a year.

He said his advocate worked between those hospitals for him, and that other members' money got him through surgery and back on his feet. No dollar figures in that one either.

For scale, CrowdHealth's published list of large funded bills includes two heart events, at $180,129 and $122,340. Its public results also show a heart attack request that went out to 224 members.

## What's the honest part?

Neither of these reviews has a final number, and the first one wasn't finished. "Many bills funded, others in negotiation" is good news. It's not the same as "done."

Heart events also raise the hardest version of the pre-existing question. If you'd been treated for a heart problem in the five years before joining, a later heart event could fall under that rule. High blood pressure and high cholesterol by themselves are exempt, which helps a lot of people my age.

And think about that wife for a second. For weeks, those bills were arriving at her house with her name on them. Nobody had signed a contract promising to pay them. She trusted a track record. It held. I just don't want to make that sound smaller than it was.

## The takeaway

Two years of paying in. One catastrophic week. A phone call the next morning, and someone saying "take care of him first." That's the moment the whole thing either works or it doesn't. For this couple, by her account, it was working.

## Questions I get about this

### Would CrowdHealth cover a heart attack or bypass surgery?

A new heart event is eligible, and CrowdHealth's list of large funded bills includes heart events of $180,129 and $122,340. You'd pay the first $500. A heart condition treated before you joined could fall under the pre-existing rule for your first two years.

### What should my family do if I'm in the hospital and can't call?

Have your spouse or a family member contact your care advocate or open an event in the app as soon as things are stable. In this story, the wife called the next day. Then collect every bill as it arrives. I'd put the advocate's contact in both your phones today.

### Is high blood pressure a pre-existing condition on CrowdHealth?

CrowdHealth's member guide says high blood pressure and high cholesterol are exempt from the pre-existing limits. Other heart conditions diagnosed or treated in the five years before joining aren't. Details in [pre-existing conditions and health sharing](/p/pre-existing-conditions-and-health-sharing/).
