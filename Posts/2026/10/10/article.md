# Kidney Stones: Funded in Under Two Weeks

> A fit business owner, eight months into CrowdHealth, needed surgery for kidney stones. He paid the bills himself first, turned them in, and the crowd paid him back in less than two weeks. Kidney stones are one of the most common requests in CrowdHealth's public results, with about 60 funded requests over roughly two years.

Folks who've had a kidney stone tend to describe it the same way: the worst pain of their life, out of nowhere. It's a classic "healthy people get surprised too" problem.

## What happened?

A man who runs a few businesses and takes his fitness seriously wrote a public review this summer. He'd been a member for eight months and hadn't needed anything.

Then the stones hit. He described severe pain, and he ended up needing surgery. He was clear that the surgery cost a lot.

He paid the bills out of his own pocket. Then he sent them to CrowdHealth. The crowd funded them in less than two weeks.

A second member, with a family and more than three years in, wrote about an ER trip for a kidney stone. CrowdHealth negotiated the bills, and the community funded what was left after the member's $500.

A third member wrote about a daughter who was in and out of the hospital with several procedures for kidney stones, and how much help they got turning in all those bills.

## What does the bill look like?

None of these three members shared a dollar amount, so I'm not going to invent one.

What I can show you is how common this is. CrowdHealth posts its funding requests each month, with the state and the type of event. Over about two years, I count roughly 60 requests for kidney stones. One was sent to 43 members. Another, for a stone removal, went to 35. An ER visit for stones went to 18.

That tells you something useful. A kidney stone bill is big to one person and small to a crowd. A few dozen people chip in and it's done. It's like splitting the check when one friend's car breaks down. Thirty-five people throwing in is a non-event for each of them.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

For the first member, mostly one thing: paid him back fast. He covered the bills, uploaded them, and the money came back in under two weeks.

For the second, they negotiated the ER bills down before anything went to the crowd.

## What's the honest part?

He had to pay first. That's the piece people don't love, and they're right to notice it. If you get hit with a surgery bill, you need either cash, a card with room on it, or a payment plan with the hospital while you wait.

This is why I keep saying an emergency fund and CrowdHealth go together. I wrote about building [the first $1,000](/p/the-first-1000-emergency-fund/) for exactly this kind of week.

CrowdHealth says that for bigger bills it can negotiate, and sometimes fund, before you pay. So ask your advocate before you put a large bill on a card.

One more thing. Kidney stones tend to come back. If you had stones or symptoms in the five years before joining, a new stone could be treated as a pre-existing condition and not shared in your first two years. A first-ever stone for a new member is a different story.

## The takeaway

A healthy guy got blindsided, paid for surgery, and was made whole in under two weeks. That's the system working the plain, boring way it's supposed to. The price of admission is being able to float the bill for a little while.

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth cover kidney stones?

A new kidney stone is an eligible health event. You pay the first $500 and the crowd shares the rest once the bill is negotiated. CrowdHealth's public results show about 60 funded kidney stone requests over roughly two years. A history of stones before joining may fall under the pre-existing rule.

### Do I have to pay the hospital up front?

Often, yes, and then you're paid back. One member with kidney stone surgery was repaid in under two weeks. For large bills, ask your advocate first, because CrowdHealth can sometimes negotiate or fund before you pay. I cover the flow in [how one health event gets paid](/p/how-one-health-event-gets-paid/).

### Should I go to the ER for kidney stone pain?

I'm not a doctor, and severe pain is worth getting checked. If it's an emergency, go. CrowdHealth's own advice for non-emergencies is that urgent care usually costs far less than an ER. My step-by-step is in [health sharing and the ER](/p/health-sharing-and-the-er/).
