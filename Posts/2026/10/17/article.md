## Why is the gap so big?

Blood tests are cheap to run. The machine doesn't know who's paying. What changes is the price list.

When a lab bills through insurance, it often starts from a high sticker price. Your share gets figured from that, and if you haven't met your deductible, you pay most of it.

When you pay cash through a discount program, you get a price closer to what the test costs to run. I wrote a whole post on [why the same blood test costs different amounts](/p/why-the-same-blood-test-costs-different/).

It's like a hotel minibar. The candy bar is $9 in the room and $1.50 at the gas station across the street. Same candy bar. One price counts on you not walking across the street.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

They gave these members a way to buy labs at the low price. You get an order from a doctor, CrowdHealth issues a voucher, and you take it to the lab.

That's it. No crowd funding involved. A $36 lab bill is way under the $500 mark, so this is just you paying a fair price.

## What's the honest part?

Two catches, both from real reviews.

**You need a doctor's order first.** One member wanted routine blood work. They were told to do a telehealth visit to get the order. That doctor wouldn't write it. CrowdHealth couldn't issue the voucher without it. So the member needed a second appointment, and called the whole thing a runaround. CrowdHealth replied that this wasn't how it was supposed to go and said they'd look into it.

**It doesn't work everywhere.** A member in New York wrote that the lab vouchers don't apply in that state. If you live there, ask before you count on it.

And one more thing I want to be straight about. Paying $36 instead of $262 is great. It isn't the crowd paying for your labs. Routine blood work is on you, at a good price. If you want all your routine care paid for by someone else, this isn't that.

Often far less than you'd think. One member paid $36 for labs that cost $262.13 through insurance the year before. Another paid $60 instead of $300 or more. Prices depend on the tests and the lab, so ask for the cash price up front.
