# What a Month of Member Stories Taught Me

> After a month of real CrowdHealth member stories, the pattern is clear. Sudden, new health events went well: $500 from the member, the rest negotiated and shared, usually within a week or two. Trouble came from conditions that existed before joining, hospitals that wouldn't negotiate, routine care, and paperwork. It's built for the big surprise, not for everything.

For most of October I've told one kind of story a day. A toddler on a medevac. A $35,000 appendix. A hernia surgery that went great and one that went nowhere. Today I'm stepping back to look at all of them at once.

## What went right, over and over?

**New, sudden problems got handled.** The broken ankle. The kidney stones. The heart that stopped. The baby in the NICU. In story after story the member paid $500 and the crowd took the rest.

**The first price was never the real price.** $35,000 became $10,600. $48,000 became $9,190. $4,142 became $1,657 with one phone call. A heart procedure was $83,655 at one hospital and $22,000 at another.

**It was usually fast.** CrowdHealth's August results show 7.72 days on average from a complete bill to money in the account. Members said one week, under two weeks, one to four weeks.

**A real person answered.** Of everything members praised, this came up most. A named advocate who texts back. In the scariest stories, somebody said some version of "take care of your family first, the bills can wait."

## What went wrong, over and over?

**Old problems.** A $10,000 bill denied over a symptom from before the member joined. This rule catches people, and it reaches further than most expect.

**Stubborn hospitals.** Bills that took six months or a year and went to collections along the way.

**Fair price gaps.** A member offered $8,000 toward a surgery quoted at $12,000 to $15,000, and pointed to a doctor five hours away.

**Routine care.** Six routine visits, one shared.

**Paperwork.** Two separate members called it a part-time job.

**Surprises.** A newborn's two-week wait. Requests approved by default. An intro price that's per person.

## What do the numbers say?

On Trustpilot, as of late September, CrowdHealth has a 4.9 out of 5 from 1,190 reviews. About 94% are five stars. Digging for the bad ones turned up 7 one-star, 5 two-star, and 11 three-star reviews. About a quarter of this month's posts came out of those.

CrowdHealth's own results say more than 99% of eligible bills get funded. They also say 3% to 5% of submitted bills are ruled not eligible each month. Both numbers matter. The second one is where the unhappy stories live.

Keep in mind that most of those reviews were invited by the company, and many of the blogs I read earn a referral bonus. So do I.

## So who is this for?

Picture a spare tire. It's great when you get a flat. It's no help at all if what you need is an oil change every month, or if the engine was already knocking when you bought the car.

After a month of stories, here's who I think CrowdHealth fits:

- You're generally healthy, and so is everyone you'd sign up.
- You can keep $500 handy, and a cushion on top.
- You're fine paying for routine care yourself.
- You'll ask for prices and keep your receipts.
- You can live with a strong track record in place of a legal guarantee.

And here's who should keep real insurance:

- Anyone with an ongoing condition that needs costly care.
- Anyone who takes an expensive medicine every month.
- Anyone who'd lose sleep without a contract.
- Anyone 65 or older, or who uses tobacco, because they can't join anyway.

## Why am I still a fan?

Because of the dad in the parking lot.

He had a newborn in the NICU and a bill for more than $240,000 on the way. He called and asked if he was in trouble. He wasn't.

I've been through the bad reviews now. I know where it's thin. I know I'm trusting a handshake. And for my healthy family of four, I'll take that handshake.

That's what being a fanboy means to me. Not that it's perfect. That I looked hard at it and I'm still in.

## The takeaway

CrowdHealth is very good at one job: turning a big, sudden medical bill into $500 and a week or two of waiting. It isn't built for old conditions, routine care, or people who need a guarantee. If you're in the first group, I think it's worth a real look. If you're in the second, I'd tell you to pass, and I'd mean it.

Thanks for reading along all month.

## Questions I get about this

### After all these stories, is CrowdHealth worth it?

For a generally healthy household that can handle $500 per event and some paperwork, the member stories are strongly positive. Slow or unclear handling of hospital bills, followed by the paperwork load and the pre-existing condition rule. Outright unfunded eligible bills were rare in what I read. Surprises about the rules were far more common than unpaid bills.
