# The $48,000 Surgery Quote That Became $9,190

> A CrowdHealth member diagnosed with cervical cancer was quoted $48,000 by her local hospital for a hysterectomy. CrowdHealth negotiated it to $9,190. Then 142 members were asked to fund it, at an average of $64.71 each. Another member used CrowdHealth's coaching to take a $45,000 surgery estimate down to $6,500.

Of everything I read for this series, this is the cleanest set of numbers. So let's just do the math together.

## What happened?

CrowdHealth uses this as a case study on its own website. A member was diagnosed with cervical cancer and needed a hysterectomy, a surgery to remove the uterus.

Her local hospital quoted $48,000.

CrowdHealth negotiated that quote down to $9,190, before the surgery.

Then they asked 142 members to help fund it. The average ask was $64.71 per member.

## What does the math look like?

- **Hospital's first quote:** $48,000
- **After negotiating:** $9,190
- **Discount:** about 81%
- **Members asked:** 142
- **Average each:** $64.71

Multiply 142 by $64.71 and you get $9,188.82. So the crowd covered essentially the whole negotiated price.

Look at those two steps separately, because they do different jobs.

Step one, the negotiating, took $38,810 off the bill. That's the heavy lifting. Step two, the crowd, split what was left so thin that each person paid about what I'd spend taking my family out for burgers.

It's like moving a piano. First you take the legs off so it fits through the door. Then eight friends each grab a corner. Neither step works without the other.

## Is there a second story like it?

Yes, and it's from a public review, not CrowdHealth's marketing.

A member needed treatment and surgery and got a written estimate of more than $45,000. CrowdHealth taught them how those fees get set and gave them the facts and percentages to push back with. The member did the negotiating personally and got a pay-in-full price of $6,500.

That's about 85% off. And notice who made the call. The member did, with coaching.

A third member needed a surgery she described as very important to her health as a woman. She picked her own surgeon. On her old insurance, she figured she'd have paid at least $7,000 toward her deductible before the plan paid anything. On CrowdHealth she paid $500, and was paid back within a week of turning in her bills.

## What's the honest part?

That first story is a case study CrowdHealth picked for its own website. It has no date and no detail beyond the numbers. It's real according to them. It's also the best example they could find, which is what a case study is.

Not every hospital will come down 81%. Some won't come down at all, and I wrote about [what happens then](/p/when-the-hospital-wont-negotiate/).

Negotiating before surgery only works when there's time. This was a planned operation. An emergency doesn't give you that.

In the second story, the member did the hard part. If you hate haggling, know that you may be asked to do some, with a coach in your corner.

And one thing I want to be plain about: a cancer found after you join is treated as a new event. A condition you had symptoms of before joining isn't shared for your first two years.

## The takeaway

$48,000 became $9,190, and 142 people paid $64.71 each. I keep coming back to how small that last number is. A bill that could sink one household is pocket change when it's shared, but only after someone refuses to pay the sticker price.

## Questions I get about this

### How much can a hospital bill be negotiated down?

It varies. CrowdHealth says it usually gets 30% to 60% off planned big bills. In these stories, one quote dropped 81% and one estimate dropped 85%. Some hospitals refuse to negotiate at all. My own steps are in [how to negotiate a hospital bill yourself](/p/how-to-negotiate-a-hospital-bill-yourself/).

### How many members chip in on one bill?

It depends on the size. A $9,190 surgery went to 142 members at about $65 each. Each member's monthly share has a ceiling, so big bills are split across more people. I explain the approval side in [how members vote to fund bills](/p/how-members-vote-to-fund-bills/).

### Can I negotiate before surgery, not after?

Yes, and it's the better time. Ask for a written estimate, share it with your advocate, and settle the price before the date. One member's hysterectomy quote was cut from $48,000 to $9,190 this way.
