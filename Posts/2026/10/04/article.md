# Why I'm a CrowdHealth Fanboy (and How I'll Prove It This Month)

I'll say it plainly. I'm a CrowdHealth fanboy. My family of four has used it since 2022, and I'll happily talk your ear off about it.

But me liking something isn't proof of anything. So this month I'm doing it differently. Every day for the rest of October I'm taking one real health situation that a real member went through, and walking through what happened. A baby. A broken ankle. An appendix. Cancer. And a handful where it went sideways, because those are real too.

## Why am I such a fan?

Three reasons, and none of them are fancy.

**I can explain it.** I pay $60 a month per person to CrowdHealth for the help: the advocate, the bill negotiating, the app. (That fee goes to $65 in January 2027.) On top of that I chip in toward other members' bills each month. That part moves around, and it has a ceiling. If something happens to one of us, we pay the first $500 of that health event. The crowd shares the rest.

**The money goes to people.** When I chip in, it goes to another member with a real bill. Not to a building with a fountain in the lobby.

**They show their work.** CrowdHealth publishes monthly results. In August 2026 members turned in 3,870 bills, and the average bill took 7.72 days to fund once the paperwork was complete. They also publish a complaints log. I've never seen an insurance company do that.

It's like a potluck. Everybody brings a dish. When it's your turn to be hungry, there's food. Nobody promises you a plate in writing, but more than 53,000 funded bills in, people keep showing up with casseroles.

## How am I telling these stories?

I pulled together public member reviews, CrowdHealth's own published member stories, and its monthly results. Then I set some rules for myself:

- **No names, no towns, nothing that points at a person.** These are people's medical lives.
- **My own words.** I retell what happened. I don't copy it.
- **Real numbers only.** If a member didn't say what something cost, I won't guess.
- **The bad ones go in too.** Slow bills, denied bills, paperwork headaches. About one post in four this month is a story where it didn't go great.
- **I say where each story came from,** at the bottom of every post.

One more honest note. I can't verify a stranger's review. When a story comes from CrowdHealth's own site, remember they picked it. I'll tell you which is which.

## What should you know before any of this?

CrowdHealth is not insurance. Nobody is legally required to pay your bill. Members are responsible for their own bills in the end, and the crowd has funded more than 99% of the eligible ones.

"Eligible" is doing real work in that sentence. Conditions you had symptoms of in the five years before joining aren't shared for your first two years, and they're capped at $25,000 a year after that. Dental work, most mental health care, and ongoing brand-name prescriptions aren't shared. You can't join at 65 or older, or if you use tobacco. I wrote up [who health sharing is wrong for](/p/who-health-sharing-is-wrong-for/) if you want that list first.

One word note. CrowdHealth calls itself healthcare crowdfunding, not health sharing, and it isn't a ministry. I checked the company out in [is CrowdHealth legit](/p/is-crowdhealth-legit/).

## Questions I get about this

### Are these real CrowdHealth members?

Yes. Every situation this month comes from a public member review, a member story CrowdHealth published, or its monthly results. I retell them in my own words with no names and no identifying details. I can't independently verify a stranger's review, so I say where each one came from.

### What does CrowdHealth cost per month?

About $60 a month per person for the advocacy fee, plus a share of other members' bills. That second part changes monthly and tops out at $140 for someone under 55. For October 2026, CrowdHealth told members the total was $165 for a single person under 55 and $555 for a family of four, before a small transaction fee. A family of four tops out at $660 a month all in, and most months come in under that ceiling. The full math is in [how much CrowdHealth costs](/p/crowdhealth-cost/).

### Is every bill funded?

No. CrowdHealth reports more than 99% of eligible bills funded, and roughly 3% to 5% of submitted bills are ruled not eligible each month, mostly for conditions that started before the member joined. I cover what happens in [what if the crowd doesn't fund your bill](/p/what-if-the-crowd-doesnt-fund-your-bill/).
