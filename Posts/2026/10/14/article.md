# Two Hernia Surgeries, Two Very Different Stories

> One CrowdHealth member had hernia surgery within three weeks of his first appointment, with staff helping him find the surgeon. Another was quoted $12,000 to $15,000 locally, was told CrowdHealth would fund $8,000, and was pointed to a doctor five hours away. The difference is a rule called fair pricing, and you should understand it before you join.

Same surgery. Same company. One five-star review and one two-star review. I think you learn more from putting them side by side than from either one alone.

## What happened in the good one?

A married man had recently left a big-name insurance plan for CrowdHealth. He'd had a hernia fixed on the other side four years earlier, back when he was insured, so he knew the drill.

This time he noticed a bulge that grew and started to hurt. Three people at CrowdHealth helped him find a surgeon and walked him through the steps in the app. He had outpatient surgery at a large hospital system within three weeks of his consultation. In at 7 in the morning, home by 10.

He wrote his review that same day, and said the crowd funded everything.

## What happened in the bad one?

A different member also needed hernia surgery. This is someone who doesn't get paid for time off work.

CrowdHealth sent this member to two doctors. Each consult cost $300. Both doctors quoted the surgery at $12,000 to $15,000. CrowdHealth said both prices were too high compared with what it considers fair, and that it would fund $8,000.

Then they suggested a doctor five hours away.

By the time the review went up, this member had spent $600 on visits, lost two days of pay, waited months on estimates, and still hadn't had the surgery. The review was kind about the advocate. The rep was great, it said, "but his hands are tied."

## Why the difference?

It comes down to how CrowdHealth decides what to fund.

They work out a fair price for a procedure in your area. If your provider's price is close to that, the whole bill goes to the crowd. If it's well above, the crowd funds up to the fair amount, and the gap is yours unless you find a cheaper provider.

Picture a friend who offers to pay for your tires. He'll cover what tires cost at a normal shop. If you pick the dealership that charges double, he'll still chip in the normal-shop amount. Reasonable for him. Frustrating for you if the dealership is the only shop in town.

CrowdHealth replied to this member publicly. They explained the fair pricing, offered to fund travel costs, and said the consult fees could be turned in separately. In a reply to a similar review, they said they don't ask members to travel past the nearest big city for procedures under $10,000.

## What's the honest part?

Where you live matters a lot. If your area has a few surgery centers competing for patients, you'll probably be the first member. If one hospital system owns your whole region, you could be the second.

There's no way around this from where I sit. The same rule that keeps my monthly cost low is the rule that left that member stuck. Members pay less because the crowd refuses to pay the inflated price. Somebody has to be the one standing in the doctor's office when that refusal happens.

I'll also say the first review was written the day of surgery. The final bills hadn't come yet.

## The takeaway

Before you join, find out what your options are near home. Are there independent surgery centers nearby? Do they post cash prices? If the answer is "one big hospital and nothing else," know that you may be asked to drive, or to cover a gap. That's a real cost, and it belongs in your math.

## Questions I get about this

### What is CrowdHealth's fair price rule?

CrowdHealth compares your provider's price to what it considers a fair local price. Bills priced 20% or more above that get flagged, and the crowd may fund only up to the fair amount. You can switch providers, travel, or pay the difference. It's one of the questions in [what to ask before you switch](/p/what-to-ask-before-you-switch/).

### Will I have to travel for surgery?

Sometimes. CrowdHealth says it's rare and mostly affects members in small towns, and that it can help with travel costs. One member was asked to consider a doctor five hours away after two local quotes came in high.

### How do I find a fairly priced surgeon?

Ask your advocate for options before you book anything, and ask each office for a written cash price. Independent surgery centers are often far cheaper than hospitals for the same procedure. I walk through the asking part in [the cash price secret](/p/the-cash-price-secret/).
