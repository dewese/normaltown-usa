# When the Hospital Won't Budge and the Bill Goes to Collections

> One CrowdHealth member's ER bill took more than six months to negotiate and went to collections after five. Another had a hospital bill sit in collections for about a year before it was paid. Both stayed members. Slow hospital negotiations are a real cost of this model, and you carry the stress while they happen.

Yesterday's ER stories were tidy. Today's aren't. If you're thinking about joining, you need both.

## What happened?

**Six months and a collections notice.** A member had an unexpected ER visit. The hospital belonged to a system that flat-out wouldn't negotiate. CrowdHealth kept at it for more than six months.

After five-plus months, the unpaid balance was sent to collections. That means the hospital handed the debt to a company whose whole job is getting people to pay.

Within a couple of weeks of that, the balance finally came down by several hundred dollars and got settled. The member gave four stars and said the advocate personally checked in along the way. Their advice to new members: expect it to take months, and don't be shocked if a collections agency gets involved.

**A year.** A household wrote that one hospital bill went to collections while it was being negotiated and was finally paid after about a year. They described the negotiating style as stretching out the timeline to wear down the price. They wanted more updates. They've also referred two other families, so they haven't soured on it.

**No sense of urgency.** A third member, three stars, said they had to chase CrowdHealth for updates, and that nobody seemed in a hurry when bills were headed to collections. Their sharpest line was that the member keeps all the risk.

## Why does this happen?

Because of how the bargaining works.

A hospital sends a huge first bill. CrowdHealth offers a fair cash price. The hospital says no. Then it's a waiting game. Whoever gets tired first gives in.

It's like haggling over a used car where the seller won't come down and you won't come up. The longer the car sits on the lot, the better your odds. But you're the one without a car in the meantime.

CrowdHealth replied to that three-star review. They admitted their communication needed to be better. They also said collections can actually help their bargaining position, that medical bills generally aren't reported to credit bureaus until they've gone unpaid for at least a year, and that they'd step in to clean up a credit report if a provider reported one during a negotiation. They said the app now shows which step each bill is on.

## What's the honest part?

All of it. This whole post is the honest part.

When CrowdHealth holds firm on price, that helps every member. It's a big reason the monthly cost is low. But the letters and the phone calls go to one person: you. The bill has your name on it. If you're someone who loses sleep over a past-due notice, a year of them would be miserable.

I can't check the credit reporting claim for your situation, and rules like that can change. Don't take my word or theirs. Look up the current rules yourself if a bill of yours heads that way.

I'll also say this. In all three stories the bill did get paid. Nobody here got stuck with it. But "it worked out after a year" is a different promise than "it's handled."

## What would I do?

Three things.

Ask your advocate for an update on a schedule, every two weeks, in writing. Don't wait to hear from them.

Keep every letter and every bill in one folder.

If you get a collections notice, send it to your advocate that day and ask what the plan is.

## The takeaway

Most bills in CrowdHealth's results fund in about a week. A stubborn hospital can turn that into six months or a year, with collections letters in between. That's the price of having someone refuse to overpay. I think it's worth it. I'd want to know about it before I joined, not after.

## Questions I get about this

### Can a medical bill go to collections while CrowdHealth negotiates?

Yes. Several members have reported it, usually when a hospital refuses to negotiate. In the stories I read, the bills were eventually reduced and paid, after five months to a year. Keep your advocate updated on every notice you receive.

### Will a bill in collections hurt my credit?

CrowdHealth says medical bills typically aren't reported to credit bureaus until unpaid for at least a year, and that it would help fix a report if a provider reported during negotiation. I can't verify that for your case. Check the current credit reporting rules yourself.

### How long does hospital bill negotiation take?

Most bills fund in about a week once complete. Stubborn hospital bills can take months. I cover the worst case, where a bill isn't funded at all, in [what if the crowd doesn't fund your bill](/p/what-if-the-crowd-doesnt-fund-your-bill/).
