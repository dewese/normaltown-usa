# Cancer, a Few Months After Joining

> A dad joined CrowdHealth and was diagnosed with stage 4 melanoma two or three months later. He was told each treatment cost over $100,000, every three weeks. He says a surgery of about $17,000 was funded right after it was done, and a later biopsy was funded before it happened. Scans afterward showed no sign of cancer.

This is the question I get most. "Fine, it works for a broken arm. What about cancer?" So here's a cancer story, with the parts that should give you comfort and the parts that shouldn't.

## What happened?

A married dad with a family of four left a job that had given his family cheap insurance. When he priced a plan on his own, it was about $2,200 a month with $120 copays. His doctor's cash price for a visit was $95. He joined CrowdHealth instead.

Two or three months later, a growth on his head that had been removed and tested as fine came back. This time it grew fast and hurt. He got three different answers from three rounds of testing. The last one, after a second opinion, was melanoma, a serious skin cancer. He says stage 4.

Treatment was planned for every three weeks, for up to two years.

Then came surgery to take out what was left of the tumor and two lymph nodes. The nodes were clear. A scan a couple of months later showed no evidence of cancer.

## What did the bill look like?

The first number he heard from his cancer doctor was $20,000 per treatment. Then a nurse corrected it: more than $100,000 per treatment. Every three weeks.

The surgery was about $17,000 by his memory.

He didn't give totals for what was negotiated or funded, so I can't tell you those. CrowdHealth's list of its largest funded bills does include a stage 4 melanoma case at $272,972. I can't confirm that's him.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

For the surgery, they had him get a written estimate ahead of time and send it in. They funded from that. He says the surgery was funded right after it was done. A later lung biopsy was funded before it even happened.

He also said nothing had to wait on an approval, and nobody rationed his scans.

Early on, a rep told him he'd be handled the same way any other member would, as long as it met the rules.

## What's the honest part?

Several things, and they matter.

**He was scared it wouldn't count.** He'd only been a member for a couple of months. And there had been that earlier growth. The story doesn't explain how that sat with the rule about symptoms before joining. It was funded. I can't tell you exactly why the rule didn't block it, and I won't pretend to know.

**His cancer doctor was alarmed** when he said he had no insurance. Expect that reaction. You'll have to explain yourself in a lot of offices.

**Other cancer stories are less tidy.** A husband wrote that the community covered "most" of the costs of his wife's cancer treatment. Most, not all. A woman with a new breast cancer diagnosis said she felt more in control of her treatment, and also that she was doing more legwork getting estimates. Her treatment hadn't started yet.

**Long treatments are the weak spot.** CrowdHealth's guide limits ongoing prescriptions to a 12-month supply and excludes some high-cost drugs. One independent writer who joined pointed out that a condition needing expensive care forever is the real risk in this model. I think he's right.

## The takeaway

A man got a terrifying diagnosis a few months after joining, and by his account the crowd came through on six-figure treatments. That's the best evidence I've found that this works for the big stuff. It's also one person's story, chosen by the company, with questions I can't answer. For cancer in particular, I'd read the member guide twice and ask hard questions before betting my family on it.

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth cover cancer treatment?

A new cancer found after you join is an eligible health event, and CrowdHealth's published list of large funded bills includes several cancers from $143,000 to $272,000. Funding isn't guaranteed, and there are limits on long-term prescriptions and experimental treatment. Read the guide before relying on it.

### What if I'm diagnosed right after joining?

New conditions are eligible from day one. But if you had symptoms in the five years before joining, the pre-existing rule can apply even without a diagnosis. That's the gray area. See [pre-existing conditions and health sharing](/p/pre-existing-conditions-and-health-sharing/).

### Do I pay $500 for every treatment?

No. Everything tied to one condition is one health event, so you pay $500 once for that cancer, not per visit and not per year. That's very different from a deductible that starts over every January. I compare them in [the $500 you pay vs the deductible you don't](/p/the-500-you-pay-vs-the-deductible-you-dont/).
