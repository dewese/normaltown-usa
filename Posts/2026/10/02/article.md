# CrowdHealth and Pre-Existing Conditions: The Real Rules

> Anything documented, diagnosed, symptomatic, suspected, or treated within five years of joining CrowdHealth gets no funding in years one and two, then up to $25,000 a year from year three on. High cholesterol and high blood pressure are explicitly exempt. If you have a real ongoing condition, this rule is usually the whole answer, and the answer is often no.

This is the rule that decides it for most people, and it's the one most reviews gloss over. So I'm going to give you CrowdHealth's actual language, not my summary of it, and then tell you what I think it means for you.

I'm a member. My family of four has used CrowdHealth since 2022. I'd rather you read this and walk away than join and find out in an emergency room.

## What the rule actually says

CrowdHealth's term is **Previously Documented, Diagnosed, or Symptomatic** conditions. Their guidelines say the crowd welcomes members with varied health backgrounds, but there are limits on funding health events tied to these conditions.

Here's the shape of it:

- The window is **five years before you join**.
- It covers anything **diagnosed, documented, symptomatic, suspected, or treated** in that window. Note "suspected." You don't need a formal diagnosis for this to apply.
- **Years one and two of membership: not eligible for funding.** Not reduced. Not eligible.
- **Year three and after: a $25,000 per year crowdfunding cap** for anything related to those conditions.

Their own examples, and they're clear the list isn't exhaustive: asthma, congenital conditions, diabetes, genetic disorders, heart conditions, sleep apnea.

Children who join CrowdHealth at birth are excepted from the congenital condition limit.

## The exemption almost nobody mentions

**High cholesterol and high blood pressure are not subject to these limits.**

I want to put that on its own line, because those two are the single most common thing a 45-year-old is quietly worried about when they read a pre-existing clause. If your doctor has you on a statin or a blood pressure pill and you assumed that disqualified you, it doesn't.

I've read a lot of CrowdHealth write-ups. Almost none of them mention this. It's in the guidelines.

## What "suspected" means for you, practically

This is the part I'd think hardest about.

The rule doesn't require a diagnosis. If something was symptomatic, or a doctor suspected it and wrote it down, it's inside the window. That means the honest question isn't "what am I diagnosed with." It's **"what is in my chart from the last five years."**

Most people don't actually know. So before you cancel anything:

1. **Request your records** from your primary care doctor for the last five years.
2. **Read what's written there,** including notes about things that turned out to be nothing.
3. **Ask CrowdHealth directly, in writing,** about anything you find. Get the answer in writing too.

That third step is the important one. Do not rely on a blog post, mine included, for a question this specific to your body.

## What this rule is actually for

It's easy to read this as the company weaseling. I don't think that's what it is, and it's worth understanding the logic, because it tells you what the product is.

CrowdHealth isn't insurance, and it isn't legally required to take all comers the way an ACA plan is. Its costs stay low because the crowd is mostly funding unexpected things. If somebody could join the month after a serious diagnosis and have the crowd pick up an ongoing treatment, the monthly ask for everybody else would climb until the thing stopped working.

That's the trade. It's cheap partly because it doesn't do this. Knowing that doesn't help you if you're the one with the condition, but it does tell you the rule isn't going to get quietly relaxed.

## Who this rule disqualifies

Be honest with yourself here:

- **Anyone with a chronic condition needing ongoing treatment.** Two years of nothing, then a $25,000 annual ceiling, is not coverage for diabetes or a heart condition. It's a partial subsidy starting two years from now.
- **Anyone in the middle of a workup.** If you're currently being investigated for something, it's symptomatic or suspected now, which puts it in the window.
- **Anyone who'd be gambling.** If you're hoping the rule won't be applied to you, don't join. It'll be applied.

If that's you, the honest answer is a real insurance plan, and I'd rather say so than take the referral. There's more in [who health sharing is wrong for](/p/who-health-sharing-is-wrong-for/).

## Who it doesn't disqualify

- Reasonably healthy people whose charts are boring.
- People managing **only** cholesterol or blood pressure, per the exemption above.
- People whose past issue is more than five years back and has stayed quiet.
- Anything genuinely new that happens **after** you join. That's the main event, and it's funded normally.

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth cover pre-existing conditions?

Not in years one and two. From year three, up to $25,000 a year for conditions documented, diagnosed, symptomatic, suspected, or treated within five years of joining.

### How far back does CrowdHealth look?

Five years before your join date.

### Does high blood pressure disqualify me from CrowdHealth?

No. High blood pressure and high cholesterol are explicitly exempt from the previously documented, diagnosed, or symptomatic limits.

### What if I was never formally diagnosed?

The rule covers conditions that were symptomatic or suspected, not just diagnosed. Pull your medical records and ask CrowdHealth in writing before you join.

### Is asthma a pre-existing condition for CrowdHealth?

Asthma is on their own list of examples, so yes, if it was documented, diagnosed, or symptomatic within five years of joining.

### What about a baby born after I join?

Children who become members at birth are excepted from the congenital condition limitation.
