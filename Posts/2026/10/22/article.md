# Torn Knees and Sprained Ankles: How Rehab Gets Paid

> One member tore her ACL a month after joining CrowdHealth and paid $500 instead of the $3,000 or more she expected on insurance. Another sprained his ankle badly and needed four months of physical therapy. After his first $500, checks arrived weekly. He did have to pay for each visit up front. Starting in 2027, therapy is limited to 15 visits per health event unless a review approves more.

Surgery gets the headlines. Rehab is where the bills quietly pile up. Twice a week, for months. So how does that part work?

## What happened?

**The ACL.** A woman joined CrowdHealth because she and her husband wanted to start a family and liked that it supports births outside a hospital. One month later she tore her ACL, the ligament that holds the knee steady.

On her old insurance, she figured the injury would have cost them $3,000 or more. On CrowdHealth, their share was $500. She said they were repaid quickly for everything past that, and she was careful to add that she stayed on top of uploading detailed receipts.

The crowd even shared the acupuncture she used while her knee healed.

**The ankle.** A man who's been a member for three years wrote on his blog about a bad ankle sprain from soccer. He opened a health event in the app on the way to urgent care. The X-ray showed no break.

CrowdHealth texted him to confirm it was a new injury, then approved it. He picked his own physical therapist, one near home who works with athletes. Two sessions a week. He was off the field for four months.

Once his bills passed $500, he started getting a check every week for that week's sessions. Nobody capped how many visits he could have.

That part is changing. Starting January 1, 2027, CrowdHealth limits physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, pelvic floor therapy, and chiropractic care to 15 visits per health event. Needing more doesn't mean an automatic no. It means a clinical review decides.

**The equipment.** A third member recovering from an ACL injury said the crowd also helped with the small extras, like the equipment needed during recovery.

## What do the bills look like?

Here's the key rule. A health event is everything tied to one injury. The urgent care visit, the X-ray, the surgery if there is one, and every therapy session after all count as the same event.

So you pay $500 once. Not $500 for the X-ray and another $500 for therapy.

Think of it like a bar tab. You open it with the first drink. Everything after goes on the same tab until you close it out. You don't start a new tab each round.

For the bigger version, CrowdHealth's public results show an ACL tear request of more than $10,000 that went out to 72 members.

## What did CrowdHealth actually do?

Confirmed the injury was new, approved it, and then paid steadily as the receipts came in. For the soccer player, that meant a check a week for months.

## What's the honest part?

**You pay each visit first.** The soccer player said it himself. He paid the therapist at every session, then got paid back. With two visits a week, that's real money floating out of your account before it floats back.

**It only works if you keep up with the paperwork.** The ACL member mentioned staying on top of her receipts. If you're the type who lets mail pile up, your money will pile up with it.

**The first weekend can be confusing.** A different member who tore an ACL on a weekend said the start was rocky. They needed answers fast and didn't yet know how to find a provider who takes direct payment. Their suggestion was weekend chat support for new folks. Fair point.

**"Maintenance" care isn't shared.** CrowdHealth's guide excludes ongoing chiropractic visits and massage. Therapy to recover from a specific injury is one thing. A standing weekly adjustment forever is another.

## The takeaway

Rehab after an injury is one event with one $500, however long it takes. The catch is cash flow. You front each visit and get paid back as you go. If you keep your receipts moving, the money keeps moving too.

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth cover physical therapy?

Yes, when it's part of recovering from an eligible injury or surgery. One member had four months of twice-weekly sessions shared after his first $500. Starting in 2027, there's a limit of 15 visits per health event unless a clinical review approves more. Ongoing maintenance care, like routine chiropractic visits, isn't shared.

### Do I pay $500 again for follow-up care?

No. Everything related to the same injury is one health event with one $500 commitment, including imaging, surgery, and therapy. I explain the idea in [how one health event gets paid](/p/how-one-health-event-gets-paid/).

### What does ACL surgery cost on CrowdHealth?

Your share is $500. The full cost depends on where it's done. CrowdHealth's founder described one member quoted about $24,000 at a hospital and about $11,000 with a surgeon who takes direct payment. Those are his recalled numbers, so treat them as rough.
