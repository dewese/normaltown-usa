# Having a Baby on CrowdHealth: What $3,000 Covers

> On CrowdHealth, a whole pregnancy is one health event with a $3,000 member commitment covering prenatal care, delivery, and postpartum. The catch is timing: your due date has to be at least 300 days after you join. Members report getting paid back in one to four weeks, and some say the billing paperwork felt like a part-time job.

Babies are the second biggest thing the CrowdHealth crowd pays for. The company expects about 800 of them this year. So there are a lot of member stories, and they rhyme.

## What happened?

I pulled four recent ones.

A young family, members for almost two years, just had a baby. They pay around $250 to $300 a month and said the whole pregnancy was funded, usually within one to four weeks of turning in each bill. They said there was no haggling over any of it.

A mom expecting twins wrote that she'd already hit her $3,000 commitment, and everything she'd paid since had come back to her within weeks.

A third mom had a rougher ride. Pregnancy was her very first health event as a member. She had to collect itemized bills, talk to billing offices, pay up front, and wait to be paid back. She said it has still paid off compared to insurance. She also said the medical billing "felt like a part time job."

And a fourth, writing on her own blog, was using a birthing center. One of her larger bills sat unreviewed for about two months during a busy season. When she raised it, it was paid right away and she got an apology.

## What does the bill look like?

Here's how the $3,000 works.

With regular health events, you pay the first $500. Pregnancy is different. It's one big event that covers everything from the first prenatal visit through postpartum, and your share is $3,000 total.

Think of it like a cover charge at a concert. You pay once at the door, and after that the show is covered, whether the band plays one set or three.

For comparison, a mom of three wrote that each of her earlier births on insurance cost her more than $6,000 out of pocket. And those were simple hospital births with no complications.

CrowdHealth says it also shares costs for midwives, doulas, lactation help, pelvic floor therapy, and one breast pump. About 40% of CrowdHealth babies are born at home and another 20% at birth centers.

## What's the honest part?

There are three rules here that will bite you if you skip them.

**The 300-day rule.** Your due date has to be at least 300 days after your membership starts. If you're already pregnant when you join, that pregnancy isn't shared. This isn't something you sign up for after the test turns positive.

**The price went up.** Older reviews talk about having a baby for $500. That was true in 2022. It went to $1,500, and now it's $3,000. If you read an old review, check the date.

**The baby is a separate person.** Routine newborn care is its own event with its own $500. If the baby has complications at birth, that's another separate event. So a rough delivery could mean $3,000 plus $500 plus $500.

And you'll likely pay bills first and get paid back. That's a real cash flow thing to plan for.

## The takeaway

For a planned pregnancy, $3,000 for the whole thing is a good deal next to what a lot of families pay on insurance. But it only works if you join well ahead of time and you're willing to keep your paperwork in order. Join first. Then plan the baby.

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth cover pregnancy?

Yes, on the standard membership, if your due date is at least 300 days after you join. Your share is $3,000 for the whole pregnancy, including prenatal visits, delivery, and postpartum care. Pregnancies that started before you joined aren't shared. More in [maternity on health sharing](/p/maternity-on-health-sharing/).

### Can I have a home birth or use a midwife?

Yes. CrowdHealth lists certified midwives, doulas, birthing classes, and home birth supplies as eligible. Its founder says about 40% of member babies are born at home. Several members said choosing their own kind of birth was the reason they joined.

### How long does it take to get paid back for pregnancy bills?

Members in public reviews report anywhere from a week to four weeks per bill. One blogger had a bill sit for about two months before it was fixed. Keep itemized bills and receipts, and plan to float some costs for a few weeks.
