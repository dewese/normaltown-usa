# Earning Bitcoin on Your Cash: River's 3.50% Interest, Explained

> River pays interest on cash you keep in your River account, and it pays that interest in bitcoin. As of October 2026 the rate is 3.50% a year, and it's variable, so it can change. Your cash stays dollars, held at an FDIC-member bank. Only the interest turns into bitcoin, a little each day.

Most checking accounts pay almost nothing. River's pitch is simple: leave cash with them, and they'll pay you interest in bitcoin instead of pennies. Here's how it actually works, and what to watch.

## The short answer

As of October 2026, River pays 3.50% APY on cash in your River account. APY means "annual percentage yield," the yearly rate including the effect of interest earning interest. The rate is variable. River set it on October 3, 2026, and it can go up or down.

The interest builds up every day, gets converted into bitcoin every day, and lands in your account once a month. If you'd rather have the interest in dollars, River lets you choose that instead.

Your cash itself doesn't turn into bitcoin. It stays dollars the whole time.

## Like credit card points, paid in bitcoin

Lots of credit cards pay rewards in airline miles instead of cash. The miles are real. But what they're worth depends on the airline, and that value moves around.

This is the same idea. Your dollars sit there earning a reward. The reward just shows up as bitcoin instead of dollars. So the amount of bitcoin you get is set by the rate, but what that bitcoin is worth next year depends on bitcoin's price.

## What the math looks like

Say you keep $1,000 in cash at River for a year and the rate stays at the October 2026 level of 3.50%. That's about $35 of interest over the year, paid in bitcoin. Roughly $2.90 a month.

For comparison, River points to a national average for checking accounts of 0.07%, from Federal Reserve data in October 2025. At that rate, $1,000 earns about 70 cents a year. River says most big banks pay about 0.10%.

Keep in mind the rate is variable, so your real number could be different.

## Where your cash actually sits

River doesn't keep your cash in its own vault. As of October 2026, cash is held at Lead Bank, which is a Member FDIC bank. FDIC insurance is the government's backstop if a bank fails. Your cash gets what's called pass-through FDIC insurance up to $250,000, which means the coverage passes through River to you.

Here's the important part. That insurance covers Lead Bank failing. It does not cover River failing, and it doesn't cover theft or fraud. And bitcoin, including the bitcoin you earn as interest, isn't FDIC insured at all.

River also says this isn't a lending product. It doesn't lend out your bitcoin to pay you.

## Getting money in and out

As of October 2026, new cash deposits take about 7 days to settle. Once your cash is settled, you can withdraw it any time, and River says it arrives within 2 business days.

That's fine for savings. It's not great for an emergency that hits tomorrow, so keep that in mind if you're thinking about [where to keep a cash cushion](/p/why-my-500-cushion-lives-in-cash/).

## The honest part

The rate is variable. 3.50% is the October 2026 number, not a promise.

The interest is paid in bitcoin, and bitcoin's price swings hard. The bitcoin you earn this month could be worth less next month, and bitcoin has stayed down for years before. If that bugs you, you can choose to take the interest in dollars.

River is a company with its own risks. The FDIC insurance covers the bank holding your cash, not River itself.

Interest is generally taxable income, even when it's paid in bitcoin. Talk to a tax pro about how that works for you.

## Questions I get about this

### Is my cash at River FDIC insured?

As of October 2026, River holds cash at Lead Bank, Member FDIC, with pass-through insurance up to $250,000. That covers Lead Bank failing. It does not cover River failing, theft or fraud. Bitcoin isn't FDIC insured.

### Does River lend out my money to pay this interest?

River says it doesn't lend your bitcoin, and that this isn't a yield product. Your cash sits at Lead Bank, and the interest gets converted to bitcoin daily.

### Do I owe taxes on bitcoin interest?

Interest is generally taxable income, and that includes interest paid in bitcoin. Ask a tax pro how to report it for your situation.
