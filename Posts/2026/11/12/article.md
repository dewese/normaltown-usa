# Buying the Dip Without Watching the Chart

> River has two tools for people who want to buy more when bitcoin's price drops, without staring at a screen. A supercharged recurring buy automatically spends extra on your regular buy day when the price is below its recent average. A Target Price Order buys once if the price hits a number you set. The first one keeps your schedule. The second one is a guess, and the price may never get there.

"Buy the dip" is one of those phrases that sounds smart and usually means staring at a price chart on your phone during dinner. A dip is just a stretch when the price falls. The trouble is nobody knows if a dip is the bottom or just the start of a bigger drop.

River has two tools that handle the watching for you. Here's how they work, and why I think one is much calmer than the other.

## Tool one: the supercharged recurring buy

A recurring buy is an automatic purchase of the same dollar amount on a schedule. I covered those in [zero-fee recurring buys](/p/zero-fee-recurring-bitcoin-buys/).

Supercharge is an optional add-on. As of October 2026, here's how River describes it. When your scheduled buy comes around, River checks the price against a moving average. A moving average is just the average price over the last several days, updated as each new day comes in. If the price is at least about 1% below that average, your buy gets bigger automatically.

You pick how much bigger: 25%, 50%, 100%, or 200%. So a $10 weekly buy with a 100% supercharge buys $20 on a dip week. On a normal week, it buys the usual $10.

The average River uses depends on your schedule, as of October 2026:

- **Hourly, daily, or weekly buys:** the last 7 days.
- **Every two weeks, or the 1st and 15th:** the last 14 days.
- **Monthly:** the last 30 days.

River doesn't charge extra for it. It's still a recurring buy.

## The grocery sale

Think about how you shop for coffee. You buy one bag every week, no matter what. But when the store runs a sale, you grab two.

You didn't stand in the aisle every day waiting for a sale. You just went on your normal shopping day and reacted to the price on the shelf. That's a supercharged recurring buy. Same day, same habit, a little extra when the price is lower than usual.

## Tool two: the Target Price Order

A Target Price Order is different. You pick a price, and if bitcoin reaches it, River buys automatically. There's also a Target Sell that works the same way in the other direction.

As of October 2026, the minimum is $10. The cash for the order is set aside until the order fills or you cancel it, so you can't spend it on anything else in the meantime. And because it's a one-time buy, the normal one-time fee applies, which is 1.00% as of October 2026.

## Why I'd lean on the first one

A Target Price Order is a prediction. You're betting the price will drop to a certain number. Maybe it does. Maybe it never gets there and your cash sits waiting for months. Maybe it blows right past your number and keeps falling.

A recurring buy, supercharged or not, doesn't depend on guessing. It shows up on schedule either way. I wrote about why I stopped trying to guess in [why I don't try to time the price](/p/why-i-dont-try-to-time-the-price/).

## The honest part

Supercharge spends more money on dip weeks. That means your total spending each month isn't fixed anymore. If you set a 200% supercharge on a $50 weekly buy, a dip week takes $150. Make sure the cash is there, and size it so the biggest possible week still doesn't hurt.

A dip can also keep dipping. Buying more on the way down feels smart until the price keeps sliding for a year. Bitcoin has fallen hard before and stayed down for years. Neither tool changes that, and nothing here promises a return.

### Is buying the dip a good strategy?

Nobody can tell you whether a dip is the bottom. A steady recurring buy avoids having to guess. If you add supercharge, think of it as "a little extra on sale weeks," not as a way to beat the market.
