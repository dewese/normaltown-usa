# A Black Friday Gift That Isn't More Stuff: Sending Bitcoin by Text

> River Link lets you send a little bitcoin as a link you can text, email, or drop in any messaging app. The person taps it and claims the bitcoin to a River account or another wallet. No wallet address or long code needed. You can even print it as a PDF with a QR code and tuck it in a card. It's a small gift that doesn't need a closet.

It's Black Friday. The ads have been yelling since Halloween. And if your house is like most, the closets are already full of last year's deals.

So here's a different idea for the nephew, the coworker, or the friend who keeps asking about bitcoin. Give them a little of it. Not as an investment pitch. As a way to learn by holding a tiny piece.

## The card with a bill tucked inside

You know the birthday card from Grandma with a twenty folded inside? The card is the wrapper. The twenty is the gift. Whoever opens the envelope gets it.

River Link works like that card. The link is the envelope. The bitcoin is the bill inside. Your person opens it, and it's theirs.

Normally, sending bitcoin means asking the other person for an **address**, which is a long string of letters and numbers where bitcoin can be received. Most people you'd gift to don't have one. They've never heard of one. River Link skips that step.

## How it works

Here's the flow, as of October 2026:

1. In the River app, choose to send bitcoin with River Link and pick an amount.
2. River makes a link. Share it however you like: text, email, any messaging app.
3. Your person taps the link and claims the bitcoin. They can claim it into a new River account or send it to another bitcoin wallet they already use.

Want something to put under the tree? River also lets you print the link as a PDF with a **QR code**, which is that square barcode your phone camera can read. Fold it into a card and you've got a physical gift.

One requirement, as of October 2026: your own River account has to be verified to send a River Link.

You don't need to give a whole bitcoin. Bitcoin splits into tiny pieces called **sats**, short for satoshis. A gift of a few dollars is a real, spendable piece. I explain that in [You Don't Need a Whole Bitcoin](/p/sats-you-dont-need-a-whole-bitcoin/).

## Why I like this kind of gift

The best way to understand bitcoin is to own a small piece of it and watch what happens. I said the same thing in [Your First $20 in Bitcoin](/p/your-first-20-in-bitcoin/). A small gift lets someone do that without spending their own money.

It also gives you an easy, low-pressure way to talk about it. "Here's a few bucks of bitcoin. If you're curious, here's a free lesson." That's it. No speech.

## The honest part

The value will change. A $25 gift might be worth $15 in a month, or $35. It might stay down for years. Say that out loud when you give it, so nobody feels misled.

Treat the link like cash in an envelope. Send it straight to the person it's for, never somewhere public.

Gifts can have tax rules, for you and for them, especially bigger ones. For a few dollars it's unlikely to matter, but if you're giving more, talk to a tax pro.

And River is a company with its own risks. Once your person claims it, they can keep it on River or move it to a wallet they control. I wrote about that choice in [Not Your Keys, Not Your Coins](/p/not-your-keys-what-it-means/).

Give an amount you'd be fine giving as a gift card. That's the right size.

## Questions I get about this

### Does the person need a River account?

No. They can claim it into a new River account or to another bitcoin wallet they already use.

### Can I give bitcoin to a kid?

Most financial accounts are for adults, so check River's rules before you plan on it. And talk it over with the parents first.

### How much should I give?

A gift card amount. Something you won't think twice about if the price drops tomorrow.
