## Why homework, and not hype

Most bitcoin content falls into two camps. One camp says it's going to the moon. The other says it's a scam. Neither one helps a busy parent decide anything.

What helps is understanding how the thing actually works. What a "sat" is. Why the dollar buys less than it used to. What a seed phrase is and why you never type it into a website. Once you understand the pieces, you can make your own call, and that call might be "not for me." That's a fine answer.

I buy bitcoin every week with an automatic recurring buy on River, and River sends it to my own hardware wallet once it hits an amount I picked. I'll explain both of those this month. But the point isn't to copy me. The point is to understand it well enough to decide for yourself.

## Think of it like learning a song

Nobody learns a whole song in one sitting. You learn the first chord. Then the second. Then how to switch between them without stopping. A week later your fingers just know it.

Bitcoin works the same way. It looks like a wall of strange words from the outside. Take it one chord at a time, though, and it stops being scary. By the end of November you'll know more than most people who own it.

## Questions I get about this

### Do I need to buy bitcoin to follow along?

No. You can read every post and every River Learn lesson without an account and without spending a dime. Learning first is the smart order anyway.

Yes. The lessons are public pages on River's website. River hopes you'll become a customer, of course, and that's why it's upbeat. But you don't pay anything or sign up to read them.

### Where should a total beginner start?

Read River's Bitcoin in 10 Minutes, keep the glossary open in another tab, and then read [what a bitcoin wallet actually is](/p/what-is-a-bitcoin-wallet/). That gives you the big picture and the words to go with it.
