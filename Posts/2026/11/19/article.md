# Most Bitcoin Is Stolen by Scams, Not Hackers. Here's the Lock I Use

> Most stolen bitcoin isn't taken by a genius hacker. It's handed over by a regular person who got tricked. The best protection is a slow door: River's ForceField puts a weekly limit on how much bitcoin can leave your account, and taking it off takes 5 days (as of October 2026). Scammers need you to hurry. A 5-day wait ruins their plan.

When people picture bitcoin theft, they picture a hoodie, a dark room, and lines of green code. That happens. But River's own security page puts it plainly: most bitcoin theft comes through scams, not hacks.

In plain English, somebody talks you into sending it.

## The con always needs you to hurry

Every scam I've read about has the same engine. Urgency.

"Your account is locked, log in here now." "This giveaway ends in an hour, send bitcoin and get double back." "Grandpa, I'm in trouble, please don't tell Mom, just send it." Someone you met online who suddenly has an amazing investment for you.

River Learn's scam lesson sorts these into a few buckets:

- **Social engineering:** a person tricks you into giving up information or money. Romance scams and fake relatives in trouble live here.
- **Phishing:** a fake email or lookalike website pretending to be your bank or your bitcoin app, trying to get your login.
- **Giveaway scams:** "send 1, get 2 back," often using a famous person's name or face.
- **AI scams:** fake voices and videos that sound or look like someone you trust.

Notice what they all need. Speed. They want you acting before you think, and before you call someone who'd talk you out of it.

## The slow door

Think about the safe at a convenience store. A lot of them have a time delay. The clerk punches the code, and the door won't open for several minutes. A robber standing at the counter can yell all he wants. The door doesn't care. The point isn't to make the money impossible to reach. It's to make it slow to reach, so a crook in a hurry gives up.

ForceField is that time-delay safe for your River account. I have it turned on for mine. Here's how it works, as of October 2026:

- You set a weekly limit on how much bitcoin can leave your account.
- Lowering the limit happens right away.
- Raising the limit, or turning ForceField off, takes 5 days. River notifies you every day during the wait.
- It doesn't affect buying, selling, receiving bitcoin, or moving cash in and out.

So if someone steals your phone, gets your password, or sweet-talks you into "sending it right now," they can only get out what's under your limit. Everything else is behind a 5-day door, and River is pinging you daily that the door is opening. That's plenty of time to realize something's wrong.

## The honest part

No lock beats a person who's been fooled for a full week. ForceField buys you time. You still have to use that time to stop and ask someone.

Bitcoin you send to a scammer is almost always gone. There's no bank to reverse it.

And none of this protects you from the price. Bitcoin can fall hard and stay down for years. River is also a company with its own risks. A lock on the door doesn't change what's in the house. Only put in money you can leave alone.

My simple rules: nobody legit will ever rush you. Nobody legit doubles your money. When in doubt, call a family member before you send anything.

## Questions I get about this

### Does ForceField stop me from buying bitcoin?

No. As of October 2026, it only limits bitcoin leaving your account. Buying, selling, receiving, and moving cash work the same.

### What if I really need to move more than my limit?

You can raise the limit or turn ForceField off. It just takes 5 days, as of October 2026. That's the whole point. A real need can wait 5 days. A scam usually can't.

### How do I spot a phishing email?

Don't click links in emails about your account. Open the app or type the website yourself. Look closely at the sender and the web address. If it's urgent and wants your login, treat it as fake until proven otherwise.
