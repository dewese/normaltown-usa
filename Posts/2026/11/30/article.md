# What Happens to Your Bitcoin If Something Happens to You

> If you die without a plan, your bitcoin can be lost for good. For bitcoin held at River, you can name beneficiaries in the app, which sets up a transfer-on-death registration for your account. For bitcoin in your own wallet, nobody can move it without your keys, so your family needs a separate, careful plan. Most people need both.

Nobody likes this topic. I don't either. But I've got a family of four, and the whole reason I save is for them. A savings plan they can't get to isn't much of a plan.

So let's talk about it plainly, and then go hug somebody.

## The safe with no combination

Picture a heavy safe in the closet. Everything important is in it. You know the combination by heart. You never wrote it down, because that felt risky.

Then something happens to you. Your family knows the safe is there. They can see it. They just can't open it. And with bitcoin, there's no locksmith.

That's the problem in one picture. Bitcoin held in your own wallet can only be moved with the secret key, usually backed up as a **seed phrase** (a list of words that can restore your wallet). If nobody can find it or knows what it is, those coins sit there forever. River Learn has a whole lesson on what happens to lost bitcoin. The short answer: nothing. It just can't be spent again.

## Two places, two plans

Think about where your bitcoin actually lives. For a lot of people, it's in two places.

**On River (or another company).** Here a company holds it, so there's an account with your name on it and a process to follow.

**In your own wallet.** Here only the key matters. Paperwork alone can't move it.

My setup has both. I buy weekly on River, and River auto-withdraws to my own cold storage hardware wallet once it hits an amount I picked. I explained that in [Auto-Withdrawals](/p/bitcoin-auto-withdrawals/). So at any moment, a little sits on River and the rest is in my own wallet. Each part needs its own plan.

## Plan one: River's beneficiary plan

As of October 2026, River lets you name beneficiaries for a personal account right in the app. You can name people or trusts. It creates a **transfer-on-death registration**, which is a way to say who gets the account when you die, a lot like the beneficiary line on a life insurance policy or retirement account.

A few details, as of October 2026:

- One beneficiary plan per personal account.
- Not available for business accounts.
- You can edit it in the app anytime.

It only covers what's on River. Bitcoin you've already moved to your own wallet isn't part of it.

## Plan two: your own wallet

This is the harder part, and River Learn's inheritance lesson is honest about why. If you share your keys too early, more people can make mistakes or get targeted. If you share nothing, your family may never get in.

The middle ground most guides point to looks something like this:

- Your family knows bitcoin exists and roughly where.
- There are written instructions, in plain English, on what to do and who to call.
- The seed phrase is stored safely and separately, and someone you trust can reach it when it matters.
- Your will mentions it, and an estate attorney has looked at it.

Write the instructions for a smart person who has never touched bitcoin. Because that's probably who'll be reading them.

## The honest part

I'm not an estate attorney, and this isn't legal advice. Laws differ by state. Talk to an estate attorney about how a transfer-on-death registration fits with your will.

A plan for your bitcoin doesn't protect its value. The price can fall hard and stay down for years, so your family could inherit less than you expect. Only save what you can leave alone.

And River is a company with its own risks. A beneficiary plan depends on River being around to carry it out.

## Questions I get about this

### What happens to bitcoin if the owner dies with no plan?

If it's on an exchange, the family usually has to work through that company's process with documents. If it's in a self-custody wallet and nobody has the seed phrase, it's likely gone for good.

### Should I just give my spouse my seed phrase?

It depends on your family. More copies mean more ways to lose it or have it stolen. River Learn's inheritance lesson walks through the trade-offs. Read it together, then decide.

### Does River's beneficiary plan cover my hardware wallet?

No. It covers your River account. Bitcoin you've moved to your own wallet needs its own plan. I explain the master backup in [Your Seed Phrase Is the Master Key](/p/what-is-a-seed-phrase/).
