# Open Enrollment Starts Today. Read This Before You Auto-Renew

> Open enrollment on HealthCare.gov runs from November 1 to January 15. Pick a plan by December 15 if you want it to start January 1. Before you let last year's plan roll over, look at the new price and ask whether you even need the same thing. For some families, healthcare crowdfunding like CrowdHealth is a fit. For others, keeping insurance is the right call, and I'll tell you who.

Today the door opens. On HealthCare.gov, open enrollment starts November 1 and ends January 15. If you want a plan that starts January 1, you need to pick it by December 15. If your state runs its own exchange, the dates can be different, so check your state's site.

Here's my one ask: don't just let it auto-renew without looking.

## Why auto-renew is the easy mistake

Auto-renew is like a gym membership you forgot you had. It keeps charging, and you never stop to ask if you still go.

Plans change from year to year. The monthly bill for insurance can change. Your help paying for it can change. The doctors in the network can change. The deductible can change. Rolling over means you agree to all of that without reading it.

So pull up next year's numbers. Write down three things for your family:

1. The monthly bill for insurance, after any help you get.
2. The deductible, which is what you pay before the plan starts helping.
3. The out-of-pocket maximum, which is the most you'd pay in a bad year.

Add the monthly bill times 12 to the deductible. That's roughly what a year with one real health event costs you. Now you have something to compare.

## Who should keep insurance

This is the honest part, and it matters more today than any other day of the year. Here's who I'd tell to stay put:

- **Anyone with an ongoing condition that needs regular, costly care.** Conditions you had before joining generally aren't eligible for crowdfunding.
- **Anyone who wants a legal guarantee.** CrowdHealth has funded a lot of bills, but it can't promise to pay any of them.
- **Anyone already pregnant.** For maternity, the due date has to be at least 300 days after your membership starts.
- **People in California, DC, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Rhode Island, or Vermont** who need coverage that satisfies their state's insurance requirement. CrowdHealth doesn't.
- **People whose help on the exchange makes a plan very cheap.** If your monthly bill is close to zero, the math may simply favor insurance.
- **Daily tobacco users and people over CrowdHealth's weight limits,** who aren't eligible to join.

If any of those is you, [who health sharing is wrong for](/p/who-health-sharing-is-wrong-for/) goes deeper. There's no shame in it. Right tool, right job.

## The one-way door

Here's the part people miss. If you drop insurance and later change your mind, you usually can't just sign back up in March. Outside open enrollment, you can only sign up through the exchange if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period. That takes a life event, like moving, losing other coverage, getting married, or having a baby.

So treat this as a once-a-year decision. Make it on purpose. If you do switch, line up your CrowdHealth start date so you don't leave a gap, and read [what to ask before you switch](/p/what-to-ask-before-you-switch/) first.

## Questions I get about this

### When does open enrollment end?

On HealthCare.gov it runs November 1 to January 15. Choose by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1. A plan picked between December 16 and January 15 starts February 1. State exchanges can set their own dates.

### Can I have insurance and CrowdHealth at the same time?

Yes, nothing stops you. Some people keep insurance for one family member with a known condition and put the others on CrowdHealth. Just do the math on paying for both.

### Does CrowdHealth have open enrollment?

No. You can join any month. That's handy, but it doesn't change the insurance side: getting back onto an exchange plan still usually waits for open enrollment.
