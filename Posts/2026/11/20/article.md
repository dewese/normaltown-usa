# Your Paycheck, Straight Into Bitcoin (and Who Shouldn't Do It)

> River lets you send your paycheck there by direct deposit and pick how much turns into bitcoin automatically, with the rest staying in dollars. As of October 2026, that conversion is free up to $30,000 a month and the cash side earns 3.50% interest (a variable rate), paid in bitcoin. It's a neat tool for someone who already has a cushion and a steady budget. If you're living paycheck to paycheck, it's not for you yet.

Some people love the idea of getting paid in bitcoin. I get the appeal. But the honest answer starts with who should skip this, so that's where I'll start too.

## Who shouldn't do it

Skip this, for now, if any of these are true:

- **You don't have an emergency fund.** If the car breaks down and your bitcoin is down 30% that week, you'll be forced to sell at a bad time. Build [the first $1,000](/p/the-first-1000-emergency-fund/) first.
- **You carry credit card debt.** Every dollar you pay down stops that interest for sure. Bitcoin promises nothing. I did the math in [the real cost of a credit card balance](/p/real-cost-of-a-credit-card-balance/).
- **Your bills eat the whole check.** Bitcoin is for money you can leave alone for years. If there's nothing left over, there's nothing to leave alone.

If that's you, there's no shame in it. Most of us have been there. Come back to this later.

## The faucet splitter

Now, for everyone else, here's the idea.

You know those splitters you screw onto an outdoor faucet? One spout, two hoses. You turn a little lever on each side to decide how much water goes to the garden and how much goes to the kiddie pool.

Direct deposit to River is that splitter for your paycheck. The water is your pay. One hose fills your dollar balance for bills. The other hose fills your bitcoin savings. You set the levers once and it runs every payday.

## How River's version works

Here's what River offers, as of October 2026.

**Direct deposit.** Your employer sends your pay to a River account number instead of a bank. River says you can get paid up to two days early. You choose how much converts to bitcoin automatically and how much stays as cash.

**Free conversion.** Turning your paycheck into bitcoin is free up to $30,000 per account per month.

**Interest on the cash.** The dollars you keep earn 3.50% APY, as of the rate set on 10/03/2026. APY just means the yearly rate with interest added on interest. It's variable, so it can change. The interest is paid in bitcoin unless you choose dollars. The cash is held at Lead Bank, and it's covered by FDIC insurance up to $250,000. That protects you if Lead Bank fails. It doesn't cover River itself failing, theft, or fraud. And bitcoin is never FDIC insured.

**Bill pay.** You can pay rent, bills, and credit cards from River, using cash or bitcoin. There's an account and routing number.

**What's missing.** As of October 2026, River's FAQ says there's no debit card, no credit card, and no checks. So for most folks, this sits next to a regular bank, not in place of one.

## The honest part

Bitcoin's price swings hard. It can fall by half and stay down for years. If the bitcoin side of the splitter is set too high, you might need that money before it recovers.

Paying a bill by selling bitcoin may be a taxable event. That means you might owe tax on any gain. Keep it simple: pay bills from the cash side.

River is a company. Putting your paycheck there means trusting it with more of your money life. The interest rate can drop. The rules can change.

Start small. Set the bitcoin side to an amount you'd never miss. You can always turn the lever up later.

## Questions I get about this

### Do I have to put my whole paycheck in River?

No. Most payroll systems let you split direct deposit between accounts. You could send a small slice to River and keep the rest at your bank.

### Is the cash in River FDIC insured?

As of October 2026, River says cash is held at Lead Bank, Member FDIC, with pass-through insurance up to $250,000. That covers a Lead Bank failure. It doesn't cover River failing, and bitcoin isn't covered at all.

### Is the interest taxable?

Generally, yes, interest is income. Talk to a tax pro about your situation.
