# Thanksgiving Table Questions About Bitcoin, Answered Calmly

> If bitcoin comes up over turkey today, four questions usually follow. Is it a Ponzi scheme? No: nobody's promising returns or paying old investors with new investors' money. Is it safe? The network has run for years; most losses come from scams, lost keys, and shaky companies. Why does it swing so much? It's still small and young. Isn't it for criminals? Every transaction sits on a public record forever, which is a bad fit for crime.

Happy Thanksgiving. I hope your table is full and the pie holds out.

If someone finds out you've been buying a little bitcoin, the questions come quick. Here's how I'd answer the big four, calmly, between bites. You don't have to win. You just have to be clear and kind.

## "Isn't it a Ponzi scheme?"

A Ponzi scheme has a boss in the middle. That person promises big returns, then pays early investors with money from new investors. When new money slows down, it falls apart. The name comes from Charles Ponzi, who ran one about a hundred years ago.

Bitcoin doesn't have that boss. Nobody collects your money. Nobody promises you anything. The rules are public, and anyone can check them. The price goes up and down because people buy and sell it, and it has gone down a lot, many times. A Ponzi scheme can't afford to show you that.

What is true: plenty of scams have used bitcoin's name. "Send us bitcoin and we'll double it" is a Ponzi pitch, not bitcoin. I wrote about spotting those in [the scams post](/p/bitcoin-scams-and-forcefield/).

## "Is it safe?"

Split this into two questions.

Is the network safe? Bitcoin has been running since 2009, with no company or government in charge of it.

Is your bitcoin safe? That part depends on you and who you trust. Most people who lose bitcoin lose it to a scammer, a lost backup, or a company that went under. That's why I keep it boring. I buy weekly on River, and River auto-withdraws to my own cold storage hardware wallet once it hits an amount I picked. More on that in [Auto-Withdrawals](/p/bitcoin-auto-withdrawals/).

## "Why does the price jump around so much?"

Here's the one picture I'd use. Picture a small fishing boat and a cruise ship on the same lake. A big wave hits. The cruise ship barely notices. The fishing boat rocks hard.

Bitcoin is still the fishing boat. It's young and small compared with the stock market or gold. So when a lot of people buy or sell at once, the price moves a lot. There's also no one whose job is to smooth it out.

That's the honest reason I only buy money I can leave alone for years. I go deeper in [Bitcoin's Wild Price Swings, Explained Calmly](/p/bitcoins-wild-price-swings-explained-calmly/).

## "Isn't it what criminals use?"

That was the reputation early on. But every bitcoin transaction is written into a public record that never goes away. Anyone can look at it, including the police. Companies now make tools that trace those records. River Learn points to estimates from firms that track this showing that illegal activity is a small slice of bitcoin use, while noting those numbers are estimates, not proof.

A permanent public record is a strange choice for someone trying not to get caught.

## The honest part

I don't know where the price goes next. Nobody does. Bitcoin can fall hard and stay down for years. If your relative's real worry is "don't lose your shirt," they're right to worry, and the answer is to keep it small and keep it boring.

River is a company with its own risks, too. That's part of why mine doesn't sit there long.

You don't need to convert anyone today. "I buy a little each week and leave it alone" is a perfectly good answer. Then pass the rolls.
