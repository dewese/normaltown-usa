# Why My $500 Cushion Lives in Cash, Not Bitcoin

> I buy bitcoin every week, and I still keep the money for CrowdHealth's $500 member commitment in plain old dollars. Emergency money has two jobs: be worth the same amount next Tuesday, and be there today. Bitcoin is great at neither of those. It's my long-term savings. The cushion is for the bill that shows up this month.

People are sometimes surprised by this. I buy bitcoin every week on River, and it moves itself to my own hardware wallet. I think it's the best long-term savings tool I've found. So why isn't my emergency money in it?

Because emergency money and savings money are different jobs. Using one tool for both is how people get hurt.

## The $500 I always keep ready

On CrowdHealth, you pay the first $500 of every health event yourself. A sprained ankle, a kid's stitches, a bad case of the flu that turns into an urgent care visit. The crowd only steps in after that.

So I keep a cushion for that $500 sitting in cash. Not invested. Not clever. Just there.

I wrote about why this matters in [the first $1,000 emergency fund](/p/the-first-1000-emergency-fund/). It's the shock absorber that keeps a normal surprise off a credit card.

## Two jobs bitcoin isn't built for

**Job one: be worth the same next week.** If the urgent care bill is $400, I need $400. Not $400 on a good week and $260 on a bad one. Bitcoin's price swings hard. It has dropped by more than half in a matter of months, more than once. That's fine for money I won't touch for years. It's terrible for money I might need on Tuesday.

**Job two: be there today.** My bitcoin lives on a hardware wallet that I control. That's on purpose. It's hard to get to, which keeps me from spending it. But "hard to get to" is the opposite of what you want when a bill is due. Selling, moving it, and waiting for dollars to land in the bank takes time and steps.

Think of it like a spare tire. The spare tire has one job: be ready when you're stuck on the side of the road. You don't make it fancy. You don't swap it for something that might be worth more later. It just has to hold air today.

## Where bitcoin does fit

Bitcoin is where my long-term savings go. Money I won't need for years. Money I can leave alone through a bad stretch without flinching.

That's the whole rule I laid out in [saving vs. gambling](/p/is-bitcoin-saving-or-gambling/). It's not about what the thing is. It's about which pile the money came from. Rent money in bitcoin is gambling. Money you can forget about for five years is closer to saving.

So the order I follow goes like this:

1. Cash cushion for the $500 commitment and normal surprises.
2. Bills paid, no credit card balance.
3. Then a small, steady amount into bitcoin every week.

The weekly buy is the part I'm writing about all month. If you want the setup, it's in [zero-fee recurring bitcoin buys](/p/zero-fee-recurring-bitcoin-buys/).

## The honest part

Some bitcoin fans will tell you dollars lose value, so holding any cash is a mistake. I agree dollars lose value over time. I've written about [the dollar's slow leak](/p/the-dollars-slow-leak/). But a few hundred dollars losing a little value over a year is a small cost. Selling bitcoin in a crash to cover a bill you knew was coming is a big one.

And if you don't have the cash cushion yet, don't buy bitcoin yet. Build the cushion first. That goes double if you're thinking about CrowdHealth. If you can't keep $500 on hand, the $500 commitment will hurt, and [who health sharing is wrong for](/p/who-health-sharing-is-wrong-for/) is worth a read before anything else.

## Questions I get about this

### How much cash should I keep for CrowdHealth?

At least $500, since that's the commitment for one health event. The $500 applies per event, per person, so a family can have more than one in a year. A bigger cushion, like $1,000 or more, gives you room for two.

### Couldn't I just sell a little bitcoin when a bill comes?

You could. But you'd be selling at whatever the price is that week, maybe a bad one, and you might owe taxes on the sale. The cushion means you never have to make that choice under pressure.

### Should my bitcoin ever be emergency money?

Not for me. Once the cash cushion is solid, bitcoin is for the long haul. Mixing the two jobs is how people end up selling at the worst time.
