**Email:**

Hey friend,

Quick question. Imagine a mechanic who doesn't charge to fix your car. He charges for every visit. He's nice. He's not a crook. But the day your car runs perfect and never needs him again is the day his paychecks stop.

Would a guy like that rush to fix your car for good?

I've been chewing on that, because it's pretty much how our health system is wired. The doctor's office mostly gets paid per visit, per test, per procedure. More pieces, more pay. And the insurance company? There's a rule that caps what they keep at about 20 cents on the dollar. Which quietly means the only way to grow their slice is to grow your whole bill.

Put it together and you get the thing you've always felt but couldn't name. Nobody in the system actually gets paid on the day you're simply well and need nothing.

This isn't about villains. Your doctor's a good person. It's about the plumbing underneath everybody. And the good news about plumbing is that it can be re-routed.

Doctors, under fee-for-service, are paid per visit and procedure. That's volume, not outcomes. Insurers, under the 80/20 rule, can keep about 20 cents per premium dollar. So their profit grows only when the total bill grows.

Follow that logic and you land somewhere uncomfortable. Nobody in the chain gets paid on the day you're simply well.
