# Fact-Check & Sources — "Nobody Gets Paid to Make You Well"

Station 4 pass. Verdicts and sources for every factual claim.

| Claim in article | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Insurers must spend ≥80% of premiums on care (85% for large employer plans); the rest is overhead/profit. | **Confirmed** | [HealthCare.gov – Rate Review & the 80/20 Rule](https://www.healthcare.gov/health-care-law-protections/rate-review/) |
| If they miss it, they owe rebates; insurers paid back close to $1B in 2024. | **Confirmed** (~$958M to 6M+ consumers) | [healthinsurance.org – ACA rebates](https://www.healthinsurance.org/obamacare/billions-in-aca-rebates-show-80-20-rules-impact/) |
| A capped slice can create an incentive to grow total spending. | **Confirmed as a real, studied concern** (not just opinion) | [RAND – Unintended Consequences of the MLR Requirement](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP71133.html) |
| Fee-for-service pays per visit/test/procedure and rewards volume, sometimes unnecessary care. | **Confirmed** | [RevenueXL – Fee-for-Service](https://www.revenuexl.com/blog/fee-for-service); [Innovaccer – FFS vs Value-Based Care](https://innovaccer.com/resources/blogs/fee-for-service-vs-value-based-care-differences) |
| CrowdHealth charges a flat $60/mo (plus variable crowdfunding contributions); makes money from the fee, not a % of claims. | **Confirmed** (2026: $60/mo advocacy fee; crowdfunding up to $140/mo under 55; family from ~$180/mo) | [CrowdHealth – Pricing](https://www.joincrowdhealth.com/pricing); [CrowdHealth – Member Guide](https://www.joincrowdhealth.com/resources/member-guide) |
| Health sharing is not insurance; no guaranteed payment; has exclusions/limits. | **Confirmed** | [CrowdHealth – Member Guide](https://www.joincrowdhealth.com/resources/member-guide) |
| Cash/self-pay prices are often lower than insured prices. | **Confirmed as common, not universal** — softened to "you'll be surprised how often" rather than "always." | General self-pay pricing reporting; verify per-provider. |

## Edits made during this pass
- Reframed the insurer argument away from "they deny claims to get rich" (the 80/20
  rule undercuts that) to the **defensible** "capped slice → incentive for a bigger
  bill." Research *strengthened* the thesis.
- Softened the cash-price claim from absolute to "often."
- Confirmed CrowdHealth number is the **flat $60 fee plus** members' bill shares —
  article says exactly that, so we don't imply $60 is the all-in cost.
