# The 5 Money Leaks Busy Families Miss

> Most families aren't broke because of one big mistake. Money leaks out through five small drips: forgotten subscriptions, bank fees, the convenience tax, spending to "save" on sales, and bills that quietly crept up. Each one has a one-minute fix, and you only need to plug one this week.

Your money probably isn't vanishing on one big thing. It's leaking out through five small ones you stopped noticing.

Think of a sink with five tiny drips under it. No single drip looks like a problem. But leave them all running and you come home to a flooded floor. Money works the same way. The little leaks are quiet, so they get ignored, and quiet is exactly what makes them add up.

Here are the five that catch busy families most, and a one-minute fix for each.

## Leak 1: Subscriptions you forgot you had

The streaming service you watched once. The app you signed up for and never opened. The "free trial" that started charging in month two.

None of them is big. That's the trap. Ten dollars here, fifteen there, and it all leaves your account on autopilot while you're busy living your life.

**The fix:** Look at last month's bank statement and circle every subscription. Cancel any you'd forgotten you were paying for. You will find at least one.

## Leak 2: Fees you didn't know you were paying

Overdraft fees. ATM fees for using the "wrong" machine. A monthly "maintenance" fee on an account just for existing. Late fees when a bill slips your mind.

Banks make real money on these, and they count on you not noticing.

**The fix:** Call your bank and ask one question. "Can you waive this fee and switch me to an account with none?" They often say yes, because keeping you is cheaper than losing you.

## Leak 3: The convenience tax

The gas station snack run. The delivery app that adds a fee, a service charge, and a tip to a fifteen-dollar meal. The coffee you buy because you were out and about.

Convenience is not evil. But paying double for it every day, without deciding to, is a leak.

**The fix:** Pick the one convenience habit you'd least miss and pause it for two weeks. Just one. See what it adds up to.

## Leak 4: Spending money to "save" money

That thing was 40 percent off, so you bought it. But you weren't going to buy it at all until you saw the sale. A discount on something you didn't need isn't saving. It's spending with a good story attached.

**The fix:** Before any "great deal," ask yourself one honest question. "Would I want this at full price?" If the answer is no, the sale isn't saving you anything.

## Leak 5: Bills that quietly crept up

Your phone bill, your streaming bundle, your insurance. Companies nudge the price up a few dollars at a time, betting you won't bother to check. Most people don't. That's the whole plan.

**The fix:** Once a year, pull up your three biggest monthly bills and compare them to what a new customer pays. Then call and ask to match it. A ten-minute call can plug a leak that runs all year.

## What to do this week

Don't try to fix all five at once. That's how people give up.

Pick the one leak that made you nod the hardest while reading. Plug just that one this week. Then come back for the next. Five small patches, one at a time, and the flooding stops.

## The takeaway

You're probably not broke because of one giant mistake. You're leaking through five small drips that feel too tiny to bother with. But tiny and constant is exactly what drains a bucket. Find your drips, patch them one by one, and watch how much stays in.
