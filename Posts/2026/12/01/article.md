# Is Dental Eligible on CrowdHealth?

> Mostly no. On CrowdHealth, the only dental care the crowd funds is one dental exam with cleaning and X-rays, used as your yearly wellness event, up to $300, with no $500 member commitment first. Everything else from a dentist is not eligible: fillings, crowns, root canals, implants, bridges, dentures, wisdom teeth removal, and oral surgery. Plan to pay cash for dental work or add a cheap standalone dental plan.

If you typed "does CrowdHealth cover dental" into Google, here's the straight answer: one cleaning a year, and that's it. CrowdHealth is built for big medical surprises, and the member guide leaves dental work out on purpose.

My family of four has been on CrowdHealth since 2022, so this is a real question in our house too. Here's exactly what's eligible, what isn't, and what we do about the rest.

## What dental care is eligible on CrowdHealth?

One thing: a **dental exam, including cleaning and X-rays, as your annual wellness event.**

Each member gets one wellness event a year. The crowd funds up to $300 of it, and you don't pay the $500 member commitment first. You pick how to use it. A dental exam and cleaning is one option. So are a physical, a kid's well visit, a women's health exam, or an eye exam.

That's the catch. It's one wellness event per person, so if you use it at the dentist, you don't also get your physical funded that year. Anything past $300 is yours.

Starting in 2027 that gets more flexible. You can submit several preventive visits in a year, up to the same $300 combined. So a cleaning and a physical could both count, as long as the total stays under $300. I went through a real family's year of routine care in [six routine visits, one shared](/p/six-routine-visits-one-covered/).

## What dental care isn't eligible?

Pretty much everything else. CrowdHealth's member guide lists dental care as not eligible, outside that one wellness event. It names these specifically:

- Fillings and any other care from a dentist, hygienist, or dental assistant
- Wisdom teeth removal
- Dental implants, bridges, crowns, and dentures
- Root canals
- Palate expanders and jaw devices
- Oral surgery

There's no separate dental add-on to buy, either. If a dentist is doing the work, plan on paying for it yourself.

This sits on the same list as routine vision and cosmetic work. I explain why those gaps exist in [what's not eligible on CrowdHealth](/p/what-crowdhealth-doesnt-cover/).

## Why doesn't the crowd fund dental?

Because dental is mostly predictable, and the crowd is for the unpredictable.

Everybody needs cleanings. Most people need a filling now and then. If the crowd paid for all of it, every member's monthly ask would climb to pay for routine work everyone already knows is coming. That's how regular insurance gets expensive.

CrowdHealth keeps the monthly number low by funding the big, rare, expensive stuff: the broken leg, the surgery, the cancer. Dental costs are usually small enough to budget for. That's the trade.

## What about a dental emergency?

This is the part to think through before it happens.

The guide doesn't carve out an exception for a dental injury. If your kid falls off a bike and chips a tooth, plan on the dentist's bill being yours. If there's also an ER visit for a cut or a concussion, that medical side is handled like any other health event: you pay the first $500 and the crowd can fund the rest.

When something happens, ask your Care Advocate which bills are eligible before you assume either way. They guide you. They don't make the calls or payments for you.

## What my family does for dental

We treat the dentist like a monthly bill we plan for, not something the crowd handles.

- **Use the wellness event for a cleaning** if nobody needs it for a physical that year.
- **Ask for the cash price.** Many dentists have a self-pay price or an in-house membership for cash patients, and it's often reasonable. Here's [how to ask for a cash price](/p/the-cash-price-secret/).
- **Consider a dental savings plan or a standalone dental plan** if your family needs a lot of work. Some families do. Most don't need much.
- **Keep the money set aside.** A few hundred dollars a year for cleanings and the occasional filling is easier to budget than a surprise.

I wrote the whole playbook in [dental and vision without insurance](/p/dental-and-vision-without-insurance/).

## Questions I get about this

### Does CrowdHealth pay for dental cleanings?

Yes, once a year, if you use your annual wellness event on a dental exam with cleaning and X-rays. The crowd funds up to $300 and you skip the $500 member commitment. Starting in 2027 you can combine several preventive visits under that same $300.

### Is wisdom teeth removal eligible on CrowdHealth?

No. CrowdHealth's member guide lists wisdom teeth removal by name as not eligible, along with oral surgery, implants, crowns, bridges, dentures, and root canals.

### Are braces eligible on CrowdHealth?

No. Orthodontic work is care from a dentist, and the guide also lists palate expanders as not eligible. Budget for braces separately or look at a standalone dental plan that helps with orthodontics.

### Does CrowdHealth offer a dental add-on?

Not that I know of. The membership doesn't have a dental option. Families who want help with dental costs usually buy a separate dental plan or a dental savings plan.

### Is vision eligible on CrowdHealth?

Same rule as dental. One eye exam can be your annual wellness event, up to $300. Glasses, contacts, and other routine eye care aren't eligible.
