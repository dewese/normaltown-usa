## The finding

Experienced referrers report that **awareness content drives reach but rarely drives
applications**, and that signups come from **decision-stage content answering one
specific objection**. The named failure mode: creators with large audiences who stay at
the awareness stage and never close a decision.

| Stage | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Top | "There's an alternative to health insurance" | Reach, few applications |
| Middle | Real stories, cost breakdowns, appointment walkthroughs | Trust, indirect |
| **Bottom** | **One specific objection, answered properly** | **Where applications come from** |

Two supporting points. Long-form wins when the question is specific: a multi-minute
explainer on how emergency-room visits are handled outperformed short clips and was the
single best performer. And decision-stage content holds attention at a far higher rate,
because the viewer arrived with the question already formed.

## The accuracy finding

Referrers report members enrolling specifically because they were read **the exact
wording from the member guide**, not a paraphrase.

That is the case for the accuracy work from a revenue angle rather than an ethical one,
and it retroactively justifies the September correction pass, when the site was claiming
37,000 members against a real figure near 16,000. It also makes the verbatim pre-existing
rules in the fact sheet, exemptions included, a conversion asset and not just a
compliance one.

Everything else described is already site policy: say plainly when CrowdHealth is the
wrong answer, disclose the referral every time, refer medical questions to CrowdHealth
rather than guessing. Nothing to change.

## Tactics worth adopting

**Ask for a reply.** The biggest reported jump came from switching from passive posting
to asking people to respond in order to receive the link, then following up individually.
Our equivalent exists on paper and is unbuilt: the "12 questions to ask before you cancel
your insurance" lead magnet in `v2-plan.md` 6.3.

**Posting a blog link into relevant Facebook groups reportedly outperforms long replies
in the comments**, with signups following the next day. We have 60+ posts and no
distribution. Cheapest unexploited channel we have, and the one place a written archive
beats short video.

**The time cost is lower than it looks.** Roughly half an hour a day covers two short
videos when they are cross-posted automatically to the other platforms.

**Track saves, not views.** A save means intent to act later, which is the behaviour that
matters for a decision this size.

## Handling rule, for future crew material

Another member's earnings are their private business. Repeating them publicly would also
be an income claim, a regulated category. Neither belongs on the site or in this repo.
Use community material as a background briefing: it tells you what is true and what is
worth writing about, then write the post in your own words from a source a reader could
check themselves.
