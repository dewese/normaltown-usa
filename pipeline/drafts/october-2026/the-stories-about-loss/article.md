# The Stories About Loss

> Several CrowdHealth members have written about miscarriages. One needed a hospital procedure, and the bill was taken care of. Another lost a pregnancy before she'd been a member long enough for pregnancy care to be shared, and still received a care package. Pregnancy costs are only shared when the due date is at least 300 days after joining.

This is a quieter post than the others. I'm including it because these members chose to write about it publicly, and because what a company does when there's nothing to sell you says a lot.

I'll keep it short and plain.

## What happened?

A family that almost never sees doctors wrote about a pregnancy that ended in a miscarriage and required a procedure at a hospital. They were new to paying a hospital directly. CrowdHealth helped them through that part, and the bill was taken care of.

Another member wrote that everything was funded quickly, and that when they had a miscarriage, CrowdHealth sent flowers and a care package.

A mom wrote about losing a pregnancy at 18 weeks. She said the team was kind and helpful.

Another had two losses in one year. She said packages arrived before she'd even gotten home from the hospital.

## What about the member who wasn't eligible yet?

One mother's story stayed with me. She had a miscarriage before she'd been a member long enough for pregnancy-related care to be shared. So that loss wasn't funded.

CrowdHealth sent her a care package anyway.

Later, in her next pregnancy, they helped her find better prices on ultrasounds. She said she saved 50% on each appointment.

## What are the rules here?

I'd rather you know these now than learn them at a hard time.

Pregnancy care is shared only when the due date is at least 300 days after the mother's membership began. A pregnancy that started before or soon after joining isn't eligible, and that includes a loss.

When a pregnancy is eligible, it's one event with a $3,000 commitment from the member. I explained that in [having a baby on CrowdHealth](/p/having-a-baby-on-crowdhealth/).

If you're hoping to grow your family, the timing of when you join matters more than almost anything else.

## What's the honest part?

Flowers don't pay a hospital bill. For the mother who wasn't eligible yet, the kindness was real and so was the cost she carried herself. Both are true.

I also can't tell you the dollar amounts in any of these. None of the members shared them, and I'm not going to guess.

What I can say is that CrowdHealth's founder has talked about how other members respond when one of these requests goes out. He said some write back asking to give double that month.

That doesn't surprise me. A crowd is made of people. Many of them have been through the same thing.

## The takeaway

Most of this month's stories are about dollar figures. These barely have any. One family's hospital bill after a loss was taken care of. One mother's wasn't eligible, because of the 300-day rule. Either way, the members who wrote about it said they were treated like people.

## Questions I get about this

### Are miscarriage costs shared on CrowdHealth?

If the pregnancy is eligible, meaning the due date is at least 300 days after joining, the costs are part of the pregnancy event. One member reported a hospital procedure after a miscarriage being taken care of. A pregnancy outside that window isn't shared.

### How long do I have to be a member before pregnancy is covered?

Your due date must be at least 300 days after your membership start date. In practice, join before you're pregnant. More in [maternity on health sharing](/p/maternity-on-health-sharing/).

### Does CrowdHealth include any mental health support?

In 2026 the standard membership includes virtual talk therapy through the app. Starting in 2027, talk therapy counts toward the yearly $300 wellness amount. Other mental health care, such as outpatient psychiatry, isn't shared. The full list is in [what CrowdHealth doesn't cover](/p/what-crowdhealth-doesnt-cover/).
