# When the Birth Plan Changes

> Birth plans change. One CrowdHealth member planned a home birth and ended up with a high-risk pregnancy and a scheduled C-section. Another had a full-term baby spend 10 days in the NICU. Both say the bills were handled. The $3,000 pregnancy commitment stays the same if things get complicated, and a newborn's complications count as a separate $500 event.

Yesterday I covered the plain version of having a baby on CrowdHealth. Today is the version where it doesn't go to plan, because that's the one that keeps people up at night.

## What happened?

**The home birth that wasn't.** A mom whose family had joined nine months earlier was planning a low-key home birth. Then her pregnancy turned high-risk. The plan became a scheduled C-section with a lot more medical care than she'd wanted. She was still a happy member when she wrote about it.

She also shared a side story I liked. Her son needed a treatment that normally wouldn't be eligible. But it was less invasive and cheaper than the surgery that would have been eligible. Her advocate made the case inside CrowdHealth, and it got approved.

**The full-term baby in the NICU.** Another mom had joined CrowdHealth as a backup while she still had insurance through work. Then she quit her job and the insurance went away. Her daughter was born full term and still spent 10 days in the NICU, the intensive care unit for newborns.

She said the NICU bills were negotiated way down from where they started, and they were still a lot of money. She paid, and then the crowd paid her back.

**The newborn who couldn't breathe.** A third family's newborn daughter landed in intensive care with breathing trouble. About a month after she came home, they wrote that all their bills had been taken care of.

## What do the bills look like?

None of these three shared dollar amounts, so I won't make any up. Here's what I can tell you from CrowdHealth's rules:

- **The pregnancy:** one event, $3,000 from you, whether it ends in a home birth or a C-section.
- **The baby's routine care:** a separate event, $500 from you.
- **Complications for the baby, like a NICU stay:** another separate event, $500 from you.

It's like a road trip. You budgeted for gas. Then the car needed a tire. The tire is a separate bill, but it's a known size, and it doesn't change what you paid for gas.

For scale, CrowdHealth's list of its largest funded bills includes several NICU stays between $126,000 and $437,000.

## What's the honest part?

The second mom said she was nervous when the NICU bills showed up. Of course she was. You're holding a huge bill with your name on it and trusting that strangers will come through. They did. But the feeling is real, and nobody should tell you otherwise.

She also had to pay first and wait to be paid back. With NICU-sized numbers, that's a serious thing to plan for. CrowdHealth says it can negotiate large bills before you pay, so ask about that early.

Timing matters too. A baby has to be added to your membership within 30 days of birth. And all of this only applies if the pregnancy was eligible to begin with, which means a due date at least 300 days after you joined.

And the approval for that boy's treatment was a judgment call. It went his way. A judgment call can go the other way too.

## The takeaway

The thing I like here is that the price to you doesn't balloon when the plan falls apart. A home birth and an emergency C-section cost the member the same $3,000. A NICU stay adds $500, not $50,000. It isn't guaranteed. But the size of your share is known ahead of time, and that's most of what I want from any plan.

## Questions I get about this

### Does a C-section cost more than $3,000 on CrowdHealth?

Not for your share. The $3,000 pregnancy commitment covers prenatal care, delivery, and postpartum care as one event, however the delivery goes. The rest of the eligible bills go to the crowd after they're negotiated.

### Is a NICU stay covered by the pregnancy commitment?

No, it's separate. Illness or complications for the baby at birth are their own health event with a $500 commitment. Routine newborn care is another $500 event. Add the baby to your membership within 30 days of birth.

### What if my pregnancy becomes high-risk?

You can change your plan, your provider, or where you deliver. One member did exactly that, moving from a home birth to a scheduled C-section. Tell your advocate early so the bills are set up right. More on that role in [what a care advocate actually does](/p/what-a-care-advocate-actually-does/).
