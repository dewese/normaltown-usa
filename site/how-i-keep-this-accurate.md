# How I Keep This Accurate

I write about medical bills and money. If I get a number wrong, somebody makes a real decision on bad information. So here's exactly how the facts on this site get checked, how often, and what happens when I find out I was wrong.

## Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this site traces back to a primary source. Not a blog post about the source. The source.

- **CrowdHealth's own pages** for anything about what it costs, what it covers, and what the rules are. Their member guide, pricing page, and FAQ.
- **Government pages** for anything about the law, taxes, or your rights. State insurance departments, the IRS, HealthCare.gov.
- **The actual study** for anything with a statistic in it, not the news article about the study.
- **My own bills and my own account** for anything about what my family has paid or had funded. Those are the numbers I trust most, and they're the ones nobody else can give you.

Posts that lean on outside numbers carry a **Sources** box at the bottom with the link and what it backs up. If a claim doesn't have a source, it's my opinion and I try to say so in the same breath.

## How often I check

- **Monthly:** the CrowdHealth facts. Cost, the member commitment, the exclusions, the pre-existing condition rules, the referral offer. These change, and a stale number here is the most likely way I'd mislead you.
- **When something happens:** if a state issues a notice, if a rule changes, if the company announces something, I go update the affected posts rather than waiting for the monthly pass.
- **Before every new post:** any number going into a new article gets re-checked against the source that day, not copied from an older post of mine.

## What the dates mean

- **Published** is the day it first went up.
- **Updated** only appears when a post got a real revision. A site-wide wording change or a template tweak doesn't stamp a new date on everything, because that would be a lie about how fresh the writing is. If you see an Updated date, something in that post actually changed.

## When I get it wrong

I will get things wrong. Here's what I do about it:

1. **Fix the post**, and put the Updated date on it.
2. **Say what changed**, in the post, not quietly. If the old version could have led somebody to a bad decision, I say what the old version said.
3. **No silent edits.** I don't go back and quietly change a number so it looks like I was right all along.

If you spot something wrong, [tell me](/contact/). I read every message. You won't hurt my feelings, and I'd rather be corrected than be confidently wrong in front of a few thousand people.

## What I'm not

I'm not a financial advisor, a doctor, a tax professional, or a lawyer. I'm a guy who reads the source documents and writes down what they say in normal words. Everything here is information, not advice. You make your own calls, and for anything big you should talk to somebody licensed.

## What I can't verify for you

Some things I simply can't check, and I try to flag them when they come up:

- **What the crowd will do.** CrowdHealth is not insurance. Nobody is legally required to fund your bill. Their track record is good and I'll show you the numbers, but a track record is not a guarantee and I won't describe it as one.
- **What your specific situation costs.** I can show you my family's real numbers and the published rates. I can't tell you what your body, your state, and your year will add up to.
- **Anything about your eligibility.** The rules about conditions you already have are specific and they change. Ask them directly, in writing, before you cancel anything.
