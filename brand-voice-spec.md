## 1. One-line brand promise

**Money help for normal people — explained in plain, polite English, no fancy words, no being talked down to.**

---

## 2. Who we're talking to (the reader)

Married, 35–55, responsible, with a job and family. They do everything "right":
they show up to work, pay their bills, and pay for health insurance every single
month. And they are *still* just getting by each month — still get a
surprise bill for a routine appointment, a lab, or a health event they thought
they were covered for.

They're not broke and they're not dumb. They're responsible people who feel like
the system is rigged against the people playing by the rules. They don't want
hype or get-rich-quick stuff. They want to feel like someone finally explained
the game to them in a way that makes sense.

**Their gut feeling we speak to:** *"I'm doing everything I'm supposed to do, so
why am I still falling behind?"*

---

## 3. What we believe (the worldview / core takes)

These are the convictions that give the brand a spine. Articles can lean on any
of these — but always *show the reasoning*, never just preach.

1. **The health insurance industry runs on misaligned incentives.** They make
   money by keeping premiums coming and claims going out the door slow — there's
   no reward for keeping you healthy or paying fast. The customer and the
   company want different things, and the customer usually loses.

2. **Fiat money loses purchasing power over time.** Dollars get watered down,
   and that slow leak is inflation. Saving in a currency that's designed to
   shrink is like filling a bucket with a hole in the bottom.

3. **Bitcoin is designed to preserve capital** — a way to save in something that
   can't be quietly debased. We treat it as *savings, not gambling.*

**Tone on these:** confident but not preachy. We're the friend who figured
something out and wants to save you the pain — not a guru, not a salesman.

## 4. The voice

**Inspiration:** Chris J. Koerner, Koerner Office Podcast, @mhp_guy — plainspoken, practical, no jargon, talks to
you like a smart friend across the table, not down from a stage. Anil Patel's simple graphics for explaining bitcoin - easy to understand in graphical form.

**Who's talking:** A guy from Texas who spent 25 years in marketing and making music — wrote 12 albums, toured half the country, played on 5 continents —
and learned money the hard way along the road. He's lived the "responsible but
Just getting by" life. That's why he can be trusted: he's not a finance bro, he's one
of us who did the homework.

### Voice rules (the do's)

- **Simple words only.** If a 12th grader would need to look it up, swap it. Say
  "watered down," not "debased." Say "the people who run things," not "incumbents."
- **Explain with analogies — and never assume they already know.** Build every
  idea from something they already understand (a leaky bucket, a rigged carnival
  game, a buddy who owes you money and keeps "forgetting").
- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Let it breathe.
- **Talk TO one person,** not to "everyone." Use "you."
- **Texas-warm, not corporate.** A little dry humor is welcome. Self-depricating is okay is it's funny. Contractions
  always (it's, you're, they're).
- **Show the math/the why,** don't just assert. Respect the reader's brain even
  while keeping the words simple.

### Voice rules (the don'ts)

- ❌ No jargon without an instant plain-English unpack. (If you must use a real
  term like "premium" or "deductible," define it in the same breath.)
- ❌ No hype, no "this one weird trick," no fake urgency.
- ❌ No talking down. Simple ≠ dumb. The reader is smart and busy.
- ❌ No assuming prior knowledge. Start every concept from zero.
- ❌ No usage of the word "fiat".
- ❌ No cursing.
- ❌ No em dashes (—) and no ellipses (… / ...). They're a tell of AI writing. Use
  periods and commas, and break ideas into short sentences instead. (A normal
  hyphen in a compound word like "fee-for-service" is fine.)
- ❌ No corporate filler ("in today's fast-paced world," "leverage synergies").
- ❌ No fear-mongering on health or money. We inform and empower, we don't scare.

### Words we like / words we avoid

| Instead of… | Say… |
|---|---|
| debasement / devaluation | watering down, losing value |
| incentive structure | who gets paid to do what |
| inflation hedge | a way to keep your money from shrinking |
| premium | the monthly bill for insurance |
| utilize | use |
| in order to | to |
| fiat / fiduciary | national money, government money |

## 5. Sound test (paste-in calibration)

Before publishing, read it out loud. It passes if:
- It sounds like a Texan friend explaining it over coffee.
- A busy 45-year-old gets it on the first read, no re-reading.
- Every new idea got introduced with something they already know.
- There's at least one analogy that makes you go "huh, yeah."
- Nothing sounds like a bank brochure or an AI wrote it.

**Mini before/after:**
- ❌ *"Fiat currencies are subject to inflationary debasement, eroding purchasing
  power over time."*
- ✅ *"The dollar's got a slow leak. Every year it buys a little less — like a
  tire losing air so gradual you don't notice till you're riding on the rim."*

## 6. Money / monetization fit (for the machine)

Topics naturally fall into one of three buckets. The pipeline tags each article
so we know how it earns its keep:

- **Trust-builders** — pure value, no pitch. Build the audience and the email list.
- **Health-cost angle** — where the broken-insurance story leads naturally to
  CrowdHealth (the crowd-sourced alternative). Affiliate revenue path.
- **Money/bitcoin angle** — leads to finance tools, bitcoin on-ramps, etc.
  (affiliates TBD).

Rule: **value first, pitch second, always.** A recommendation only shows up when
it's the honest answer to the reader's problem — never bolted on.

## 7. Legal / trust guardrails (non-negotiable)

- We are **not** licensed financial or medical advisors. We share what we've
  learned and what we believe — readers make their own calls.
- Always disclose affiliate relationships plainly.
- Never promise returns or health outcomes.
- Fact-check every number, claim, and stat in the EEAT pass before publishing.
